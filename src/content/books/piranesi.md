# Synopsis

Piranesi lives in the House. Perhaps he always has. In his notebooks, day after day, he makes a clear and careful record of its wonders — the labyrinth of halls, the thousands of statues, the tides that thunder up staircases. There is one other person in the House, a man called The Other, who visits twice a week and asks Piranesi to help him with research into A Great and Secret Knowledge.

# Chapters

## 1 · Piranesi

He describes the House and his life in it. Completely matter-of-fact about tides flooding the lower halls. The voice does all the work here — you trust him immediately, which is the trap.

## 2 · The other

The Other appears. Shiny shoes, expensive clothes, no curiosity about the House itself. First hint that he knows something Piranesi doesn't.

# Feelings

I finished this in two sittings and then sat still for a while. It is about the House, but it is really about what someone does to a person to make them useful.

The best trick is that it's told in the voice of someone whose innocence you spend the whole book grieving in advance.
