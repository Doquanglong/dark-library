# Synopsis

Having recovered from his grievous injuries, Warmaster Horus leads the triumphant Imperial forces against the rebel world of Isstvan III. Though the rebels are swiftly crushed, Horus's treachery is finally revealed when the planet is razed by virus bombs and Space Marines turn on their battle-brothers in the most bitter struggle imaginable.

# Chapters
## 1 · The Emperor protects, the naysayers silenced, and the music of the spheres

Titus Cassar preaches to a Lectitio Divinitatus congregation in an abandoned munitions hold deep in the Vengeful Spirit, opening with a line that echoes the old joke: "I was there the day that Horus turned his face from the Emperor." Kyril Sindermann watches from the back, envying Cassar's certainty because he has felt none himself for months. He reflects on how Euphrati Keeler, after facing the warp-horror in the archive, has become the cult's first saint — witnessed by Ing Mae Sing and a dozen arms men — and how the movement is now spreading through the Expedition fleets, to the Warmaster's fury. Cassar's sermon answers the obvious question: if the Emperor is a god, what does he do with his power? He fights the dark things in the warp, he creates wonders on Terra, and above all, the Emperor protects. Then shots ring out down the corridors, the meeting is raided by Astartes, and Cassar drags Sindermann away towards engineering.

On the strategium, Horus questions Ing Mae Sing, Mistress of Astropaths. She reports that within days the gathering warp storms will obscure the Astronomican, the Navigators will be unable to guide the fleets true, and the galaxy will be a place of night and darkness. She has had no confirmation from Sanguinius, no word from Leman Russ about his campaign against the Thousand Sons, and nothing from Mars, which angers Horus because coordination with the Fabricator General is essential. Guilliman has confirmed Calth as his muster point. Lorgar's reply carried residual symbols of pride and obedience, almost fanatical. Sing admits what troubles her: every message she sends for the Warmaster is wreathed in the weight of blood and death, like breathing fiery smoke. Horus tells her to trust him — then asks her to tell him about Euphrati Keeler, the one they are calling the saint.

Mersadie Oliton and Sindermann visit Loken in the training halls, where he now trains alone. Mersadie complains about Maloghurst's edict confining remembrancers to their quarters and the Retreat, with permission required from something called the Office of the Lupercal's Court that nobody can get a reply from. Loken says they are on a war footing. Sindermann pushes further: Ignace Karkasy is dead, and Sindermann has never known a man more convinced the galaxy needed to hear what he had to say; Petronella Vivar is gone too, and not back to Terra. He states it plainly — those who oppose the will of the Warmaster are being killed, just as in the Uranan Chronicles, just as under the Overlords of the Yndonesic Dark Age, and just as at the start of the Age of Strife when the doubting voices fell silent. Loken does not refute him.

Sindermann then confesses what happened in Archive Chamber Three: the creature he and Keeler brought through with the Book of Lorgar was sorcery, the one thing he has spent his life preaching is a lie, and Euphrati stood against it unarmed with nothing but her faith. Mersadie protests that the Imperial Truth must have another explanation. Sindermann answers with the old story of the philosopher who said the universe rides on crystal spheres driven by a vast machine, and who, when told everyone would hear a machine so huge, replied that we are born with that noise around us and are so used to it we cannot hear it at all. He tells Loken, shivering and in tears, that he is starting to hear the music of the spheres. Loken finally offers what he knows: Varvarus was killed deliberately, by one of their own bolts. Then Maloghurst arrives with a dozen Army soldiers whose unit and rank badges have been stripped off — among them Maggard, visibly and unnaturally larger than before. Maloghurst tells Loken that civilians only question and complain, and that the Emperor is a long way from here. As he is marched away, Sindermann calls back that if Loken wants to understand more, he should read *The Chronicles of Ursh* again.

## 2 · Perfection, four Legions for one system, and what we do best

Saul Tarvitz and Lucius take Deep Orbital DS191 from the orks in a display of the Emperor's Children's flawless coordinated war. Lucius, whose once-perfect face now bears a neat grid of self-inflicted scars, complains that there is no sport in killing greenskins and no way to achieve perfection against such poor specimens. Tarvitz warns him not to underestimate an enemy that took a compliant world and attacks as though war were a religion. Lucius tells him he thinks too much, which is why he will never rise as high.

Fulgrim arrives to address the Legion — pale, white-haired, wearing the eagle's wing that the Emperor granted his Legion alone, and a golden-hilted sword at his hip said to be a gift from the Warmaster. He praises the victory and then delivers the orders: the Legion will be divided. He will take a small force to join Ferrus Manus and the Iron Hands at Callinedes IV; the rest will rendezvous with the Warmaster's 63rd Expedition at the Isstvan system under Eidolon, who will act in his stead. Eidolon tells them they are to put down a rebellion at Isstvan alongside the Death Guard and the World Eaters, and jokes about what a grim and artless business war becomes in such hands. Tarvitz and Lucius both find the scale strange: four Legions for one system is a sledgehammer to crack a nut. Tarvitz's deeper unease is that the Crusade, meant to push ever outward, is now turning back on itself to patch cracks — and he wonders what could possibly be left of Isstvan after four Legions have marched through it.

Confined to his quarters, Sindermann tries to reconstruct from memory the books lost in the archive fire, and finds himself accepting, with something like relief, that gods exist and that the Emperor moves among them. Then he realises his pen is moving on its own. It writes: *She needs you.* Visions come unbidden — the Warmaster in newly forged black plate with an amber eye and claws sliding from his gauntlets, lit by a ghastly red glow; then Euphrati on her medicae bed with yellowed talons tearing at the image. Someone is sending him a message. The saint is in danger.

The four fleets translate into the Isstvan system: the Vengeful Spirit, now repainted from Luna Wolves bone-white to Sons of Horus grey-green with the Eye of Horus on its prow; the Death Guard's unadorned gunmetal Endurance; Angron's blunt Conqueror; and the purple-and-gold Andronius. Sindermann talks his way past his guards with a grandfatherly performance and a gamble on Maloghurst's name, and gets a chit for the medicae deck. Elsewhere, Loken and Torgaddon walk through the Museum of Conquest and agree that the remembrancers are next, that Karkasy and Vivar are already dead, and that Torgaddon broke with the lodge when Abaddon spoke of silencing Karkasy as though it were the same as killing an enemy in war. They meet Khârn of the World Eaters, Angron's equerry, admiring a captured battle-glaive. Loken says the Warmaster taught them to remember their enemies or never learn; Khârn says there is nothing to gain from knowing your enemy, only from destroying him. Loken calls the World Eaters butchers, and Khârn cheerfully agrees — his primarch is not ashamed of what he does best, so neither is he. He tells Loken that if he had to kill every living thing in a city to win, he would do it, and that all the Legions know it while the World Eaters just preach it openly. He has heard rumours of Isstvan III: something religious, witches and warlocks, monsters from the warp. As he leaves, he says he will see them on Isstvan III — if there is anything left of it when the World Eaters are done.

## 3 · Horus enthroned, the saint is in danger, and Isstvan III

Lupercal's Court is a new addition to the ship, designed by Peeter Egon Momus to seat Horus on a great basalt throne. Loken notices banners he does not recognise: a throne of skulls against a brass tower rising from a blood-red sea, and an eight-pointed black star in a white sky. Abaddon and Aximand stand on one side of the throne, Loken and Torgaddon on the other, and Loken realises the Mournival is finally and irrevocably dead. Horus tells the two of them their role is simply to observe.

The delegations arrive. Angron — huge, scarred, with cortical implants jutting from his scalp and cables running to his collar — greets Horus by asking whether he is a subject now. Eidolon comes in Fulgrim's stead. Mortarion arrives with unpainted Terminators and Captain Nathaniel Garro, whom Loken knows and likes. Horus raises a hologlobe of Isstvan III, a world brought to compliance thirteen years earlier by Corax's 27th Expedition, and Mortarion explains that four months ago the Death Guard picked up a weak, badly delayed distress signal, probably sent at least two years before. They play it: a terrified woman in a stone chamber says it is open revolt, that the people have rejected everything, that they thought the Warsingers were primitive superstition but it was real, that Praal has gone mad and the Warsingers are with him. She fires at something off-screen, says she thinks she is the last one, begs them to send anyone — and then a hideous atonal keening kills her on camera.

Horus names Vardus Praal, the governor left to manage compliance and dismantle the planet's religious structures, as an objective. The plan divides the Choral City: Mortarion and the Death Guard will engage the main army in its western trenches; the Emperor's Children and World Eaters will take the Precentor's Palace; the Sons of Horus will take the Sirenhold, the spiritual heart of the old pagan religion. Praal is to be killed, not captured. Eidolon asks why not simply bombard the city. Horus answers that when you stamp out vermin from afar, some survive; they must get their hands dirty and destroy them in one fell swoop. Abaddon adds that the Isstvanians will be "illuminated as to the error of their ways" — and Loken is surprised by the respect in his voice for Eidolon, wondering what has changed. Angron objects that they should hit the city with everything at once, that rebellion is in the people, and that killing the people ends the rebellion. When Eidolon tries to correct him he threatens to kill him. Horus slaps both down: Angron is not beyond his authority, and Eidolon is not among equals. Afterwards Horus orders a Stormbird prepared to take him to the Conqueror, because Angron must be "illuminated" as to the proper conduct of this endeavour.

Meanwhile Jonah Aruken drinks by the leg of the Dies Irae, torn between the faith Titus Cassar keeps pressing on him and his career — he knows that if it came to a choice, he would pick the Titan every time. Cassar finds him and says the saint is in danger. Elsewhere, Maggard walks the corridors with his Kirlian blade drawn, his body swelling with Apothecary-implanted organs beyond anything House Carpinus ever did to him, his armour too small until the Legion artificers reshape it. He recalls killing Karkasy at Maloghurst's order, and remembers that the Warmaster later killed his former mistress Petronella Vivar, for which he is forever in Horus's debt. Now he has been ordered to kill a saint. Sindermann, waiting nervously, is joined by Cassar and Aruken, and the three head for the medicae deck.

## 4 · Sacrifice, a single moment, and keep her safe

In a stone-walled lodge temple deep in the ship, built in the image of the Delphos chamber where Akshub cut his throat, Erebus prepares a ritual. Horus arrives in a magnificent suit of obsidian Terminator armour gifted by the Fabricator General, with an amber eye on the breastplate and a bladed gauntlet on one hand. Regulus is brought in as the Mechanicum's witness, leading Ing Mae Sing. Erebus says he can begin once the sacrifice is made, and that it must be her — she is powerful, and a sacrifice is not a sacrifice if it is not valued by the giver. Horus tells her she betrayed him: after she told him of Euphrati Keeler, she sent a warning to someone. He offers her life for the name. She refuses, saying she is already dead, and adds that he may have the power to conceal his lies but his serpent does not. Horus rams his clawed gauntlet through her chest and lets her blood drizzle into the fire.

Erebus chants from the Book of Lorgar and something answers — a chorus of voices from everywhere and nowhere, calling itself Sarr'Kell, Lord of the Shadows. It offers power: no army will stand before Horus, no ambition be denied him. Horus is unimpressed by the word "gods" and demands plain speech. Sarr'Kell explains that the Emperor's grand designs on Terra are tearing the warp apart from within, that the powers care nothing for the material realm, and that all they want is for his works to cease and for Horus to take his place. Asked whether they can match the Emperor's Legions, Custodians and Sisters of Silence, it answers that as the Emperor creates, the warp decays and destroys, and that the victory of the gods is as inevitable as the passing of time. Horus realises they caused the warp storms — Sarr'Kell confirms it, calling it necessity, since the darkness blinds the Emperor to their plans and Horus will be given passage when he needs it. Horus does not pledge immediately. He says he will first take his Legions through the fire of Isstvan alone, because only then will they be tempered into his blade aimed at the Emperor's heart. Sarr'Kell's masters accept. Afterwards Horus sends Regulus by fast ship to Mars, with orders for Kelbor-Hal to purge his order and unite the Mechanicum, and tells Erebus to make his own preparations, because all this will be for nothing if Guilliman's Legion is allowed to intervene.

At the medicae deck, Aruken argues on the stairs about why a saint would need three men with pistols to save her, and says he wants command of a Titan, not a court martial. Sindermann answers that a long life teaches you it always comes down to a single moment in which a man finds out who he really is — and asks whether this is a moment Aruken will be proud of or one he will regret. Aruken relents. They reach Keeler's cell and find her untouched — and then see Maggard at the end of the corridor with his sword drawn, smiling a smile that promises a slow death. Cassar picks Keeler up, drips still trailing from her arms. Aruken fires twice and misses; Maggard rolls from cover and fires three times.

Nothing hits. The bullets hang frozen in the air, a bloom of muzzle light expanding infinitely slowly, another round just emerging from the barrel. Behind them Euphrati Keeler's eyes are open in terror, her right hand extended, the silver eagle burned into her flesh glowing. Then her eyes roll back and she goes limp again, but Maggard stays frozen. Aruken asks what to do about him. Sindermann refuses to let them kill him — he will not have the saint's first act be a killing, and if they are founding a new church in the Emperor's name it will be one of forgiveness. They decide instead to hide her among the faithful and keep her safe.

## 5 · Dark Millennium, and the Warsinger

Loken has found Sindermann's note tucked into the dust jacket of *The Chronicles of Ursh*: even the Warmaster may not deserve his trust, and he should look for the temple, somewhere that was once the essence of the Crusade. Standing on the abandoned strategium, he realises this is that place — and smells incense, the same bitter-blossom scent as Davin. He traces it to the banner of Targost's Seventh Company, pulls it aside, and finds a newly cut passage behind it leading to a door marked with a skull that whispers to the killer in him. Inside is a temple: stone benches, painted symbols, skulls hanging from the ceiling trailing smoke, and an altar with a bowl of dried blood and an old fire-blackened book. The script is the same as the tattoos on Erebus's skull, and as Loken looks the words twist through language after language.

Then Torgaddon's voice speaks behind him — but the face is ruined, lipless and metal-toothed with a burning yellow eye and an eight-pointed star gouged into the temple, in ornate black armour. It accuses him of trespass and betrayal, and shows him things: a dead world with a brass tower bursting from a burning hive city; the galaxy dying, stars bleeding into the void, a great red eye vomiting fleets and monsters; the Sons of Horus in black armour among capering deformities, Abaddon among them, and Horus as an obsidian giant crushing worlds. Loken insists it is a diseased vision, and the voice tells him it is the future. He finds himself in the burning archive, hands blackening as he tries to save the books, seeing through the flames to the churning warp — braying goat-faced things, bloated maggot-ridden monsters eating dead stars, a brass-clad giant bellowing from a throne of skulls, and magicians sacrificing billions in a silver city built of lies. He breaks free by screaming the words he threw at Aximand at the Delphos Gate: he will not bow to any fane or acknowledge any spirit; he owns only the empirical clarity of Imperial Truth. The temple slams back into place. What he feels is not fear but anger — and the understanding that everything he was told about the warp was a lie. He seizes the book and takes it.

On Isstvan Extremis, Tarvitz and Sergeant Fulgerion's veterans storm what intelligence called a monitoring station, with Garro's Death Guard hitting the western approach. Eidolon leads from the front, shattering the blast doors with a hammer. The Isstvanians inside wear black armour with silver scrollwork, hooded faces with a single red lens, and fight with rapid las-fire. Once inside, Tarvitz sees that the intelligence was wrong: this is not a comms station but a temple, built around a stepped pyramid made of blocks quarried from other buildings, still carrying friezes and gargoyles. At its summit floats a woman, robes lashing, singing — hundreds of harmonies grinding over one another, tearing stones from the pyramid and lifting Astartes into the air to be pulled apart. Garro is badly hurt, one leg severed at the thigh, and Tarvitz drags him clear; Garro gasps that it is a Warsinger and tells him not to listen. Captain Odovocar, the Legion's banner bearer, is lifted up and torn apart, his helmet ripping his head off with it. Tarvitz feels the song's terrible beauty, the promise of peace if he simply gives in, and forces it off.

Eidolon reaches the summit and is beaten to his knees. Tarvitz charges, is swatted down, but buys a moment's distraction — and Eidolon opens his mouth and unleashes a scream of his own, not beautiful but simply agonising, deafening volume. It shatters the Warsinger's control, and Eidolon picks up Tarvitz's fallen broadsword and takes her head off with one sweep. He hands the sword back, thanks Tarvitz for the intervention, and calls it strength of will. When Tarvitz presses him, Eidolon snaps that what he did was kill their enemies, understood. Below, the surviving Isstvanians sit weeping and rocking like children who have lost a parent. Tarvitz finds Garro alive and unwilling to admit he is hurt, and the two exchange their usual traded insults — barbarous Legion, pretty boys — as honour brothers who have saved each other's lives too often for it to matter.

## 6 · The soul of the Legion, everything will be different, and abomination

Loken summons Abaddon to the observation dome and throws the stolen book at his feet. He says he knows Erebus conspired against them, stole the anathame from the interex and brought it to Davin. Abaddon says what happens in the Crusade happens for the good of the Imperium and that the Warmaster has a plan. Loken asks whether the plan requires the murder of Varvarus, Karkasy and Vivar; Abaddon laughs and calls the remembrancers lesser people sent by the Council of Terra to strangle the Legion's ambitions. He says the Mournival was a noble idea while it lasted and that he regrets ever bringing Loken in. Loken says the lodge has brought something evil into the Legion, something from the warp, the thing that killed Jubal and took Temba, and that Erebus is lying to all of them. Abaddon tells him to forget it until after Isstvan, because after Isstvan this will end. Loken says others will stand against them; Abaddon says then they will die too. But Loken also offers a way back — for his Legion and his Warmaster, there is always a way back, as long as Abaddon feels the same.

Sindermann addresses a dangerously large gathering of the faithful crammed into a maintenance space, guarded by armed believers. Word of Keeler's rescue has spread through the ship with wild embellishments. He tells them honestly that the saint still sleeps but that she woke for one moment to save his life. He warns that Maloghurst regularly sends troops to break up their meetings and that people have already been killed. Then he poses the question he has been building to: if the Emperor protects his faithful, who protects the Emperor? Cassar, planted in the crowd on Sindermann's instructions in a classic iterator's trick, answers that they must — but that they must stay alive to do it. Sindermann tells them the Emperor has an enemy here, and they must survive to stand against it when it reveals itself.

Aboard the Andronius, Tarvitz asks Eidolon in the Gallery of Swords how he killed the Warsinger. Eidolon tells him bluntly that he is a file officer and nothing more — Fulgrim looked upon Eidolon and saw a leader, and looked upon Tarvitz and did not. Then he takes him down a hidden stair behind a mosaic in the apothecarion, into a laboratory of glowing cylinders holding half-formed Astartes bodies, a head with grown insect eyes, and linked brains with tumorous extra lobes. Apothecary Fabius explains that Eidolon's power is not psychic but a tracheal implant combined with alterations to the gene-seed rhythms. Tarvitz is appalled that the gene-seed, the blood of their primarch, has been altered — and Fabius asks who he thinks ordered it. Eidolon invokes the Cleansing of Laeran, where Fulgrim saw what the Laer achieved through chemical and genetic manipulation in their drive for physical perfection, and says the Legion must evolve to keep meeting the standards Fulgrim and the Warmaster demand. Tarvitz calls holding a xenos race up as an example of perfection an abomination. Eidolon offers him a place in the Legion's future; Fabius offers to work wonders upon his flesh. Tarvitz refuses, says it is wrong and asks whether they cannot feel it, and is ordered to say nothing and return to his men. Leaving, he cannot shake the feeling that he has just been tested — and does not know whether he passed or failed.

Loken and Torgaddon meet in the burnt-out archive. Loken says the Great Crusade was once about bringing illumination and is now afraid of knowledge, because to those who want to control people, books are dangerous. Torgaddon tells him to go and fight and put things in perspective. He also passes on something odd: the Warmaster has picked the speartip not by company but squad by squad, and Loken and Torgaddon are leading it despite everything. Loken will have Locasta with him. He says that after Isstvan everything will be different.

## 7 · The God Machine, a favour, and subterfuge

Titus Cassar runs pre-battle checks linked into the Dies Irae, noting that Mechanicum adepts in cloaks have filled the bridge with equipment he does not recognise and that every system is running better than ever. He has come to regard the Titan as the Emperor personified, an avatar of divine wrath, and murmurs that the Emperor protects — and destroys. Aruken warns him to be careful who hears, and reports that Princeps Turnet has been asking questions about how deep the cult runs among his crew, as though they were a conspiracy. He argues that they are a good team and the Dies Irae needs them; Cassar says his faith allows no compromises, and that it is Jonah's faith too, he just does not know it yet. The drop is in twelve hours, supporting the Death Guard.

On the embarkation deck, more than a third of the Sons of Horus assemble for the drop. Loken picks out Sergeant Lachost's communications squad, Apothecary Vaddon checking his narthecium and reductor, and Iacton Qruze lecturing new inductees. Vipus offers Loken a place in Locasta's drop pod and he takes it. Then Loken goes to Qruze, who has no place at all in the order of battle, and asks a favour: to seek out Mersadie Oliton, Euphrati Keeler and Kyril Sindermann and make sure they are safe. Asked why mortals matter to him, Loken says they keep him honest and remind him of everything the Astartes ought to be. Qruze agrees, saying he feels in his bones there is something big just over the horizon that they cannot see.

Aboard the Andronius, the Emperor's Children hold their traditional pre-battle feast, saluting the victory before it is won, with Lucius loudly leading the toasts and Eidolon standing in for Fulgrim. Tarvitz slips away by flattering Lucius into telling the Murder story himself. He goes instead to the Hall of Rites and finds Ancient Rylanor, the Legion's Ancient of Rites, interred in a Dreadnought after wounds beyond the Apothecaries' skill. Tarvitz asks to take over Captain Odovocar's role as Eidolon's senior staff officer aboard ship, relaying orders to the surface, framing it as a tribute to the dead man. His real reason is that the whole drop feels wrong: Eidolon, who never passes up a chance to display his prowess, is not leading it personally, and the units chosen appear picked at random with only one thing in common — none belong to Chapters led by Eidolon's favoured lord commanders, which would be an unheard-of insult. Rylanor, who is said to detect a lie the instant it is told, agrees to have him replaced, and tells him to guide them well, in a tone that leaves Tarvitz certain the Ancient wants him to stay aboard.

Deep in the engine decks, Mersadie finds Sindermann keeping vigil over Keeler in a cramped dormitory that some believer gave up for them. He tells her Maggard nearly killed him and the saint saved them. She admits she cannot believe, not yet, and asks what believing means to him. He says it gives him something to hold on to, that he has never been more terrified in his life, and that faith is easy for him because he saw what the saint did — it is hardest for her, who has seen nothing. Then white streaks cross the porthole: drop pods falling from the Andronius. As they watch the speartip driven home, Mersadie whispers Loken's name.

Below, the Choral City waits — a city older than the Imperium, built around the Precentor's Palace and dominated by the Sirenhold, whose tomb-spires carry carvings of Isstvan singing the world into being, and of the Lost Children who forsook their father and were banished to the fifth planet to become jealous monsters brooding in black fortresses. The people have armed and followed Vardus Praal's orders without knowing why, and the Warsingers have sung a formidable web of trenches into being on the western marches — the only direction from which an invasion could come. Or so the soldiers manning them had been told. The first omen is a fire in the sky at dawn: falling stars that smash into the trenches, and the word races round the city that the Lost Children have returned. They are proven right when the drop pods burst open and the Death Guard emerge.

## 8 · Soldiers from hell, butchery, and what is loaded in the guns

Loken rides down in Locasta's drop pod, thinking of what the assault will look like to the people of the Choral City: warriors from another world, soldiers from hell. Vipus hates drop pods and would rather have a Stormbird, but Horus chose this deliberately, reasoning that thousands of Astartes smashing into the defenders' midst without warning would be more psychologically devastating.

At the Precentor's Palace, Lucius kills his first foe without breaking stride — a palace guard in armour like shimmering glass, his face behind a stained-glass mask. The guards do not run or beg; they charge, and Lucius is delighted to face a foe with backbone. Their halberd-guns spit searing silver needles that bubble stone like acid. He fights his way through with Squad Nasicae and Ancient Rylanor covering with an assault cannon, into a vast indoor seascape under a granite dome where thousands more guards wait, some in glass, some in elaborate silver, some wrapped in rippling silk. He asks where the damned World Eaters are; Rylanor says they will have to win the palace themselves, which pleases Lucius because it means shaming them.

On the palace's southern side, at the Temple of the Song — a memorial to the music that Father Isstvan sang to bring all things into being — the World Eaters under Captain Ehrlen land expecting trained soldiers. Instead, Warsingers appear on the battlements and sing, and the whole civilian population of the city comes pouring into the plaza armed with whatever they had at home. Ehrlen orders a firing line, and the first ranks are cut down, but the mass climbs over its own dead. He sees the unreasoning hatred in their eyes and knows this will become a massacre — and massacre is the one thing the World Eaters excel at.

Loken's drop pod clips a stone pier and lands wedged two hundred metres up in the Sirenhold's battlements. Locasta all survive, but he watches another pod ricochet off a tomb-spire and fall in a shower of broken statues, and says bitterly that someone is going to pay for this. The vox is a mess. He orders a regroup at a spire near the western wall and they find a hidden stair behind a statue, leading down into catacombs packed with skeletons in wall niches. Casto burns something in the dark that is gone when they look. They reach a great tomb chamber with a black granite sarcophagus, its carved face featureless except for triangular eyes of coloured glass — and through all that stone Loken can hear the mournful rise and fall of a Warsinger.

Back in orbit, Tarvitz finds the Andronius at battle alert though the Isstvanians have no fleet, and the ship moving when its only assigned job is to launch the second wave. The only reason to move after a launch is to drop into low orbit for a bombardment. He works his way down to the gun decks, past the grand halls where he might be recognised, and finds the cannons being loaded and the crews sweating over them. A Mechanicum adept, taking him for an officer on inspection, reassures him that everything proceeds as planned: all bombardment cannons are loaded with airbursting warheads carrying virus bombs, as specified in the order of battle, to be launched before the second wave deploys. Tarvitz keeps his face still and sends the adept away. Virus bombs — weapons so terrible that only the Warmaster, or the Emperor before him, could sanction them, unleashing the life eater virus that strips every shred of organic matter from a planet within hours. He realises it would take many ships to saturate a world, so the order can only have come from the Warmaster, who has chosen to exterminate a third of his own warriors in one stroke. He runs for the embarkation deck to warn them.

## 9 · The power of a god, regrouping, and honour brothers

On the strategium, the company banners have been taken down and replaced with those of the warrior lodge, and the Book of Lorgar rests on a lectern where the Warmaster once briefed his officers. Horus watches the battle on pict-screens, reflecting that he cares nothing for Vardus Praal — the rebellion simply gave him the chance to rid himself of those who would never follow him to Terra. Erebus brings news from Prospero: Magnus lives, though the spires have fallen, and the warp still echoes with the sorcery he used to save his warriors and escape. Horus muses that Magnus was nearly his deadliest enemy, perhaps as dangerous as the Emperor himself, and that now he has no choice but to follow them to the end. If Fulgrim brings Ferrus Manus into the fold, he says, they have as good as won. Looking at the slaughter on the screens, he says the Isstvanians believe the gods have come to destroy them and in a way they are right — life and death are his to dispense, and what is that if not the power of a god?

In the Sirenhold, Loken and Torgaddon regroup with Sergeant Lachost among the shrines. The speartip is shattered; Loken says they will re-forge it there. He refuses to sit still, because the catacombs underfoot would let the enemy surround them, and orders them to take the tomb-spires one by one. He observes that this is more than a burial ground — whatever happened to Isstvan III started here, and this is the enemy's church. Torgaddon says madmen love their gods.

Tarvitz, flying a stolen Thunderhawk, stalls the Andronius on the vox and then tells them to go to hell. Eidolon works out immediately what he is doing — he is trying to warn them, he thinks he can make a difference — and orders fighters launched to shoot him down. In the engine decks, Mersadie tends Keeler while the faithful among the crew bring food, water and paper, kneeling meekly near the saint's bed. Then Keeler wakes screaming; when Sindermann and Mersadie touch her they are both plunged into a vision of black seething death, tendrils of darkness rotting flesh from bone, their own hands crumbling away. Keeler tells them, in a suddenly strong voice, that this is betrayal, that it is happening now, and that Kyril must tell them all.

Garro, denied a place in the Death Guard speartip because his new augmetic leg has not yet meshed with his nervous system, sees an Emperor's Children Thunderhawk crossing the Eisenstein's engagement zone with fighters on its tail, says it smells like trouble, and orders an intercept. Tarvitz recognises his voice with relief. He tells Garro plainly that they are betrayed and that the fleet is about to bombard the surface with virus bombs. Garro has seconds to decide. He weighs the impossibility of what he is hearing against the fact that Saul Tarvitz is his sworn honour brother from the Preaixor Campaign, a man who never exaggerates and never lies — and looks at the eagle Tarvitz once carved into his vambrace. The Eisenstein shoots the fighters down. Garro asks whether he was right to do it, since if Tarvitz has spoken falsely he has condemned himself too. Tarvitz reminds him of what Garro once told him about Terra: that the Emperor took a blasted world scarred by the Age of Strife and founded the Imperium there, and that is what Garro fights for. That, Tarvitz says, is what is being betrayed. Garro will tell the Andronius he shot Tarvitz down, then try to get the Eisenstein out of the system to warn the other Legions, reasoning that Rogal Dorn or Magnus would never forsake the Emperor. He asks why men have to die every time one of them tries to do what is right; Tarvitz answers that this is the Imperial Truth. They part, promising to meet again on Terra.

## 10 · The most precious truth, Praal, and Death's Tomb

Sindermann, now called the saint's apostle, addresses thousands in a sub-deck, his words spliced by the faithful into the ship's vox-caster network so they will be heard throughout the fleet. He discards his notes and speaks. The Warmaster has betrayed the Emperor. He describes Keeler's vision — fields of the dead, wind carrying a dust of bone, air heavy with blood — and asks when was the last time they approached a world with anything but murder in their hearts. What, he asks, should cultures expect from men named "Warmaster," "Widowmaker" and "the Twisted," marching to the sound of cocking bolters and roaring chainswords? He points to the Lupercal's Court, where they hang the weapons of war as curios and forget the lives they took. The dead should be their teachers, because only they know the ever-repeating failure that is war. He ends by calling on the battlefield dead to illuminate them with the most precious truth they can learn: that there must be peace instead of war.

In the palace throne room — full of organs, bells, harps and caged choristers, all playing the holy music of the Warsingers — Lucius fights his way to the dais and faces a golden-armoured warrior with a spear that screams, and speaker tubes fanning from his back. He cuts through the tubes and is hurled from the dais by the blast, and as the music washes through him he feels it in his blood, promising glories and unfettered excess, and knows he wants it more than anything in his life. Praal calls him deaf to the music of the galaxy, says he has listened to the sound of the gods and that they damn this galaxy in their wisdom, that the Imperial Truth will bring a future of fear and hatred, and that he has fulfilled his duty by bringing them here to this cauldron of fates. Lucius, with the song of death behind his eyes, can see every move before Praal makes it. He shatters the spear, drives his sword two-handed through Praal's chest, and twists it. Behind the throne he finds not a man but a servitor with twenty-fingered hands playing the control panel from the mounted books. Furious, he smashes the panel, and the music of creation ends in a howling death shriek across all Isstvan.

In the Sirenhold, Loken fights inside what they will later call Death's Tomb: a spire whose floor is sunk into a pit brimming with corpses, dominated by a black iron mausoleum topped by a statue of Father Isstvan, the sky-god who takes the souls of the faithful and casts the rest into the sky with his Lost Children. A Warsinger perches on the statue's shoulder, screaming, and hundreds of Isstvanian soldiers charge with their mouths jammed open in her cadences. More than two thousand Sons of Horus pile in. What fills Loken is not disgust at the blood but loathing at the waste — these people could have accepted the Imperial Truth and helped forge a united galaxy, and instead were turned into fanatics by a corrupt leader and are dying for a lie.

Maloghurst interrupts Horus with news that Sindermann has become a leader, an apostle, and has just broadcast a speech to the fleet calling on them to resist the Legion. Horus observes that Sindermann was chosen for this fleet precisely because he could convince the most fractious rabble of anything, and that mixed with religious fervour that makes him dangerous — they have underestimated what a civilian with genuine faith can do. He orders that the next he hears of this will be that they are all dead.

Torgaddon's firing line brings the Warsinger down against the mausoleum, and the enemy withdraws — but Loken suspects there is more to it. Lachost reports that they have lost contact with orbit entirely, as if the Vengeful Spirit were not up there. He has reached Ehrlen of the World Eaters, who is in the middle of a massacre and can barely say anything except that he is killing them with his bare hands. Nothing from Lucius. Loken orders them to break out through the western gates and link up with the rest of the strike force.

Meanwhile a decree circulates authorising all remembrancers to gather in the main audience chamber to witness the final triumph on Isstvan III, framed as the Warmaster's gesture to close the gulf that has opened between Astartes and remembrancers. Mersadie says it is obviously a trap to flush the three of them out; Sindermann half agrees and half wants to see. Then Keeler, awake and stronger than she has been in a year, says they go. She knows it is a trap and says so — but she will not have the Church of the Emperor die in shadows and murder, and Mersadie and Sindermann have to *see* this treachery to understand it. She tells them it is time to stop thinking like remembrancers: the Imperial Truth is dying, and they either die with it or follow the Emperor. Sindermann says he will follow her, and Mersadie finds herself nodding.

## 11 · The warning, the death of a world, and the Half-heard

Tarvitz lands on a palace roof and finds Lucius, swaggering, telling him the palace is theirs and Praal is dead by his own hand. Tarvitz tells him a virus strike is coming and that they are betrayed by their own — that they were sent down here to die, and that Fulgrim chose those who were not part of the grand plan. Lucius refuses at first, bitter and petulant: the primarch would not send *him* to die after all he has fought, he was one of Fulgrim's chosen, he never faltered and would have followed Fulgrim into hell. Tarvitz says he wouldn't, and that Lucius is his friend. They split the warning — Tarvitz to the World Eaters, Lucius to the Sons of Horus and Death Guard — with orders to say only that a virus strike is inbound and to find shelter. Lucius names it: this is rebellion.

Aboard the Dies Irae, in the middle of grinding the Isstvanian trenches apart alongside the Death Guard, Princeps Turnet suddenly orders the guns cold, the coolant ducts closed and the whole Titan sealed airtight, telling his crew the Isstvanians have an unknown weapon — a lie Cassar can hear. Cassar protests that the Astartes are still out there. Turnet tells him he has his orders. The Titan falls silent while the Death Guard fight on below.

In the Vengeful Spirit's audience chamber, the fleet's entire complement of remembrancers has gathered. Sindermann thinks it looks exactly like an iterators' rally on a compliant world, and looks for planted claques in the audience. Horus tells them they said they wanted to see war — well, this is it — and pict screens show the butchered streets. Then the view pans up into the heavens, and burning spears of light fall towards the planet. Sindermann names them: bombs.

The virus bombs burst at multiple altitudes across Isstvan III. The World Eaters, furthest from cover, suffer worst; corrosive agents laced into the viral structure eat through armour joints and battle damage, and Astartes dissolve into rancid soup inside their own plate — some of them after sealing themselves in bunkers, having carried the virus in with them. The civilian population dies where it stands, flesh sloughing from bone. Whole kingdoms that survived Old Night die in minutes without knowing why. Sindermann watches a black ring spread across the visible face of the planet, leaving grey desolation. Keeler tells Mersadie she has already seen it: the Emperor showed her, through Keeler. It is death. She tells them both that they are remembrancers, and must remember this and pass it on, because someone must know.

In the Sirenhold, Loken orders everyone underground through a catacomb entrance shaped like a monstrous stone head with a passage down its throat. As the dark closes over him he hears screaming from beyond the walls — the song of the Choral City's death. Tarvitz, having lied to Ehrlen that the Isstvanians have a bio-weapon because it is the only way to move the World Eaters, gets some of them into the blast-proof bunkers at the edge of the trench system just as the first streaks fall.

Keeler then leads Sindermann and Mersadie not away from the Astartes lining the chamber but straight towards one of them — Iacton Qruze. She greets him by name and asks for help. He recognises them, and says that Loken asked him to look out for them, and that he thinks he understands now what Loken meant. Keeler tells him he is the Half-heard no longer: his voice will now be heard louder than any other in his Legion. She tells him to remember Cthonia, the honour and valour from which the Luna Wolves were forged, and that he is the only one left who still embodies what it is to be an Astartes — that his counsel goes unheard because his is the voice of an age when the Crusade was noble and done for the good of humankind. Qruze, torn between loyalty to his Legion and loyalty to the ideals that made it, mutters that Loken had said "nothing too arduous," and leads them out past the door guards on his own authority as a superior officer.

## 12 · Cleansing, let the galaxy burn, and Angron's landing

Above the dying Choral City, Ancient Rylanor stands sealed inside his Dreadnought body on the palace roof, watching the viral carnage seethe between the buildings and a Warsinger fall from the sky coughing black plumes — the one note of peace in the screaming horror.

In the bunkers, Ehrlen demands to know how long they must hide like rats. Tarvitz explains the virus will burn itself out quickly, because it is designed to eat everything living and leave a battlefield for the enemy to take. Ehrlen blames himself and his Legion, saying that the World Eaters must always go further than the enemy, and this time the enemy went further than they did — a lesser soldier pretends his failures are his commanders', an Astartes knows they are his alone. Tarvitz tries to tell him the truth and is cut off. Then he warns that there is one more stage still to come: the firestorm.

In the audience chamber, Horus tells the remembrancers that this is war, this is cruelty and death, this is what the Astartes do for them while they turn their faces from it. Then, as his warriors close and seal the doors, he says the Great Crusade is over, its ideals dead, everything they fought for a lie — and that he will bring the Crusade back to its rightful path and rescue the galaxy from the Emperor's abandonment. He tells them there is no place for the likes of them in the new crusade, smiles the smile of an angelic executioner, and orders: kill them all. The slaughter takes less than a minute. Abaddon reports the virus mostly burned out and the gaseous levels off the scale. Horus imagines the planet as the frayed end of a fuse that will ignite the galaxy and lead inexorably to Terra, and gives the order: let the galaxy burn.

A lance strike from the Vengeful Spirit ignites the atmosphere. The air itself catches, and firestorm sweeps the globe — continents seared to bare rock, cities exploding as their gas lines go up, flesh and stone and metal vitrified. Any Astartes who survived the virus above ground are consumed. Billions die. Inside the sealed Dies Irae the temperature climbs until Cassar is sure he will be cooked to death in the machine he loves, and then it levels off and they live. Aruken spots gunships descending on the ruins: World Eaters colours. Cassar assumes it is the second wave. Turnet says flatly that there is no second wave, and has Aruken raise not fleet command but the Warmaster.

Qruze leads Sindermann, Keeler and Mersadie through the ship. Sindermann grieves for the remembrancers left in the audience chamber, so many creative people killed by those with no understanding of art. Keeler grows visibly stronger while Mersadie weakens. At the embarkation deck Qruze picks out Thunderhawk Nine Delta — and standing before it is Maggard.

In the ruins, Ehrlen finally asks Tarvitz the direct question: this was not the Isstvanians, was it? Tarvitz admits it. Then World Eaters gunships strafe their own position, and Angron himself leaps down with two chainaxes into the midst of his former brethren, thousands of Warmaster-loyal World Eaters behind him.

Horus puts his fist through the pict-screen. Angron has disobeyed a direct order. But his anger turns on himself: Angron is a killer who attacks first and thinks later, and Horus should have foreseen exactly this — he swears there will be no twists of fate he does not see coming again. Abaddon wants Angron destroyed with the rest of the city; Aximand argues the World Eaters are too effective a weapon of terror to waste; Erebus says Angron is predictable and can be kept obedient by being let off the leash occasionally, which prompts Abaddon to snarl that the Word Bearers may live by treachery and lies but in the Sons of Horus you are loyal or dead — and Erebus to retort that he knows secrets that would destroy Abaddon's mind, and that this reality, all Abaddon knows, is the lie. Horus shuts them down. He asks why the Emperor made him Warmaster and rejects Maloghurst's flattery; Abaddon answers correctly: because he never loses. Horus explains that he never loses because he sees only victory — on Davin he fell and came through stronger, against the Technocracy he used the war to purge dissent — and Angron's ground assault will be forged into a triumph that echoes into the future. He orders a full assault on the loyalists, with Sedirae and Targost leading the Legion rather than Abaddon, who is told he and Little Horus have another role, one he will relish. And he tells Erebus to stay out of the way.

## 13 · Maggard, factions, and the Luna Wolves

Turnet receives his orders and tells the moderati to open fire — not on the Isstvanians, who are dead, but on the Death Guard, who have defied the Warmaster. Cassar asks the question that has been forming since the seal order: the Isstvanians did not do this, did they — the Warmaster did. Turnet smiles and reaches for his sidearm, and both men draw and fire.

On the embarkation deck, Qruze and Maggard duel. Maggard's armour turns bolter shells and his enhanced strength is greater, but he is slower, his new body not yet worn in. He opens Qruze's stomach and gouges his side. When Maggard catches Qruze's knife hand inches from his throat, Sindermann picks up the fallen pistol, walks up to the struggling pair, presses the muzzle into the wound in Maggard's leg and fires. Qruze drives the knife up through Maggard's jaw into his brain. Standing over the body he says: face to face — not with treachery, from a thousand miles up. He remembers Cthonia, where they were gangs of killers like a thousand other worlds but believed in a code more precious than life, and says that is what the Emperor saw and wrought into the Luna Wolves. He had thought that even if none of the rest remembered, the Warmaster would. Keeler tells him he is the last one. Qruze admits he told them what they wanted to hear, tried to be one of them and succeeded, and almost forgot everything. He agrees to fly them off the ship, which is dead to him now.

Turnet escapes the Titan's bridge through the dorsal door when Aruken hauls the motor controls and throws Cassar off his aim. Cassar and Aruken are left alone, and Cassar makes his case: the Emperor is a god, there is a saint and there are miracles, and Aruken saw them too. He says the Emperor judges men by the choices they make when those choices are hard, and that if nobody takes a stand the Warmaster wins and there will not even be a memory of the betrayal. Aruken says he has not believed in anything for a long time, that he is sorry — and then draws his pistol. Cassar says that to know the galaxy is starved of belief, to think you might be the only one who believes, and to believe anyway: that is faith, and he wishes Aruken could understand it. Aruken fires three times.

On the ground, Tarvitz — a Space Marine who knows fear but is trained to master it — runs from Angron. The primarch cuts loyal World Eaters in half with single sweeps, and the two halves of the Legion tear at each other with more hatred than they ever showed an enemy. Ehrlen goes down, face torn to a red mask, and is carved up by a pack of his own former brothers. Tarvitz fights his way clear towards the palace, promising that if he dies here it will be among his own battle-brothers, defying the hatred the Warmaster has sown.

At the Sirenhold, Loken watches the Sons of Horus landing and can no longer think of his own men by that name. He tells Torgaddon he never imagined it ending like this — he had pictured some alien citadel or the defence of Terra, something the remembrancers could get their teeth into, not a hole like this against his own brothers. Torgaddon argues for withdrawal: they can break through the main gates and link up with the Emperor's Children and World Eaters at the palace, since Lucius said the warning came from Saul Tarvitz. Loken wants to make a stand and punish them here; Torgaddon tells him to really think about *why* he wants to fight them here, and Loken concedes that he must fight with his head and not his heart. Torgaddon says someone underestimated them and expected a massacre — instead they will turn Isstvan III into a war. Loken addresses his warriors: bonds of brotherhood have been shattered, the name Sons of Horus has no meaning for them now, and they are once again the Luna Wolves, soldiers of the Emperor. They move out two thousand strong for the gates, with armour and a Dreadnought grinding towards them across the shrines. Loken finds he feels no sadness at fighting his brothers — only hatred.

Aboard the fleeing Thunderhawk, Mersadie realises they have nowhere to go among a field of hostile ships. Keeler tells Qruze to head *towards* an ugly, unpainted gunmetal cruiser, which he does without protest. Keeler tells Mersadie the fight must sometimes be open warfare and sometimes be fought with words and ideas, that each of them has as much right as the Warmaster to a say in the fate of the galaxy, and that believing that is how they will beat him. When hailed, Keeler warns Qruze to be truthful, because all depends on it. He gives his name as Iacton Qruze, formerly of the Sons of Horus, and says he can no longer be party to what the Warmaster is doing. After a long pause the voice welcomes him aboard — and identifies itself as Captain Nathaniel Garro of the Eisenstein.

## 14 · Until it's over, Charmosian, and Lucius makes a deal

Months into the siege, Loken and Tarvitz keep watch from a broken dome of the Precentor's Palace, under a permanent ash-grey sky caused by the extinction of almost all life on the planet. The survivors — Luna Wolves and Emperor's Children mixed together — man barricades built from priceless sculptures. Loken asks how long they can hold; Tarvitz says until it is over, because every second they survive increases the chance that the Emperor hears of this and sends the other Legions. Loken wonders why the Warmaster does not simply pull his Legions out and bomb the city to dust. Tarvitz gives the answer: four primarchs, none of them given to backing down. Who would be the first to leave — Angron? Mortarion? And Horus has never shown weakness where his brothers might see it. So they will have to kill them all. Elsewhere, Mortarion landed a huge orbital lander on the western trenches and saturated the defences, and nothing has been heard from the Death Guard since — though haphazard artillery shells still land in the traitor camps daily, so some loyalists are clearly still fighting.

Lucius holds the Warsingers' Chapel and reads the battle by ear alone, distinguishing World Eater war cries from those of the Sons of Horus and telling from the sound of chainaxes that they are hacking at barricades rather than ceramite. He is sour about Solathen's praise of Tarvitz, feeling his own achievements — killing Praal, getting his men to shelter — overlooked. When he spots the glint of gold and Eidolon's Emperor's Children advancing with Land Raiders and Predators, he savours facing warriors he can respect, or earn the respect of. He holds the chapel's fire until the assault ramps drop, then leads the counter-charge, and Eidolon's force is hit from two sides.

Then Lucius picks his target: not just any warrior, but Chaplain Charmosian, in black-trimmed armour with a skull-faced helm, directing the battle from a Land Raider hatch. Lucius challenges him by name — once your brother, now your nemesis. Charmosian tells him they taught him too much pride, and swings a crozius too heavy to block, so Lucius lets it slide off his blade and frustrates him into putting more strength into each blow until the weapon is held too long inert. Then he cuts through both of Charmosian's forearms and takes his head, catching it and holding it up for the battlefield to see. The loyalists win the battle, and Lucius finds he does not care, because he has won his own — and has what he needs.

Afterwards Tarvitz finds him cutting fresh scars into his own face to remember killing Charmosian, and lets it pass as a petty concern amid so much betrayal. But when Tarvitz explains the reality — that every attack whittles them down until they cannot hold everywhere at once — and says he will send a squad to help keep watch, Lucius takes it as an insult and snaps that Tarvitz should go off and lead the big fight, being the hero. Alone, Lucius nurses his bitterness: he was the one earmarked for greatness, not Tarvitz, and his glories have been overshadowed by plodding leadership. He picks up Charmosian's helmet, digs out the communicator, and finds the channel to Eidolon still open. He tells Eidolon that a great many Emperor's Children, Sons of Horus, World Eaters and Death Guard will die before the palace falls, and that by the time the other Legions arrive Horus may have lost too many to win. Then he corrects Eidolon's assumption that he is taunting him: he wants to make a deal. He will give him Tarvitz and the Precentor's Palace.

## 15 · No shortage of wonders, old friends, and a perfect failure

Horus broods alone on the strategium, the back wall of which has now been removed to reveal a temple adjoining the bridge. What should have been a massacre has become a war he cannot afford. He remembers Maloghurst reporting that Sindermann, Oliton and Keeler were not among the bodies in the audience chamber — Maloghurst checked every corpse himself — and that they escaped by Thunderhawk to the Eisenstein, helped by Iacton Qruze, with Maggard killed in the struggle. Horus laughs mirthlessly: they have seen no shortage of wonders, but the Half-heard growing a conscience may be the greatest. The Eisenstein tried to run the blockade, was heavily damaged, and made an emergency warp jump that Maloghurst doubts it could survive. Horus is not so sure, and says that if it carries word to Terra all may be lost, because the Isstvan V phase is critical. When Maloghurst suggests withdrawing and blockading the planet, Horus shouts that he does not back down from a battle and that there are goals in the Choral City that Maloghurst cannot comprehend. Then Eidolon calls with good news: they have an ally inside the palace — Lucius.

Loken surveys the aftermath of Angron's fiercest attack yet, in what was once a garden with summerhouses and ornamental lakes. Torgaddon relinquished the forward barricade deliberately, letting the World Eaters die for it and strung them out, and by the time Loken drew his sword only momentum kept them coming. Tarvitz brings news: Lucius's communications specialists have broken the Sons of Horus communiqués, and Abaddon and Aximand are coming down to lead the Legion and bring the Warmaster's own wrath to the city. They are landing at the Mackaran Basilica, a temple with a trident-shaped spire. Loken realises what this means — deprived of the last of its Mournival, the Legion would begin to unravel — and smiles cruelly. This changes everything.

Lucius slips out of the palace, crawling through filth and past sniper positions, which humiliates him even as the sights and smells arouse him. He meets Eidolon, who calls him a traitor twice over. Lucius offers victory, and argues that Eidolon can end the siege now and keep his Astartes alive for the real battle when the Emperor answers Horus's treachery. His price is simple: he wants to rejoin the Legion, because he will not stay a captain forever like "that wretch Tarvitz." He offers to kill Tarvitz personally, complaining that the glory should be his and that Tarvitz — once content to trudge alongside his warriors and leave better men to the glory — has chosen this battle to discover his ambition. What finally hooks Eidolon is the promise of a victory so sudden it will be his alone, with the World Eaters and Sons of Horus floundering in his wake.

Nero Vipus asks to come with Loken and is refused: this is Mournival business. Loken explains his reasoning plainly — the palace will fall with or without them, so their one goal on Isstvan III is to hurt the Warmaster, and killing the last of the Mournival does that. Vipus asks whether they are on their own now; Loken says he does not know, but will not fight on blind hope alone — he is going to make a stand. Tarvitz comes to say the diversion is ready, and tells Loken formally that this may be the last time they meet. Loken says there is no "maybe" about it. They part with "For the Emperor." Loken finds Torgaddon waiting; each asks the other whether he is ready and each says no, and Torgaddon laughs that they are quite a pair.

Aboard the Vengeful Spirit, Fulgrim returns having failed to sway Ferrus Manus, whom he calls a dull fool who would not listen to reason. Horus rages that he planned Isstvan III on Fulgrim's assurance and now has another enemy, and that many Astartes will die for it. Fulgrim is changed — insouciant, sly and mocking, in ever more elaborate armour, his hair in plaits and the beginnings of tattoos on his cheeks. Horus orders him to Isstvan V to prepare the alien fortresses for the final phase. Fulgrim recoils at being made a housekeeper for someone else's grand entrance and suggests sending Perturabo — who, Horus notes, is already preparing to lay waste to his own home world in Horus's name — or Mortarion. He goes so far as to call the order a betrayal of the principles for which he joined. Horus reminds him what betrayal really means, and then points to the sword at Fulgrim's waist: the blade Horus gave him as a symbol of trust, the weapon whose true power only they know, the one that almost killed him. Would he give such a thing to someone he did not trust? Fulgrim recognises the flattery for what it is, laughs, asks whether it is working, admits that it is, and agrees to go. Eidolon will keep command until they reach Isstvan V.

## 16 · The enemy within, the Eightfold Path, and honour must be satisfied

Eidolon's Emperor's Children come into the palace through the eastern sector — Lucius's sector — and fall on the wounded first. Eidolon crushes Apothecary Vaddon's skull mid-operation and takes particular relish in killing the loyalist Emperor's Children among the casualties. The defenders find their flank turned and the fighting collapses into a battle without lines, each Astartes an army of his own.

Luc Sedirae and Serghar Targost address thousands of Sons of Horus among the rusted spires, beneath new lodge banners: a brazen claw crushing a world, a black star casting eight rays, a two-headed winged beast on a mountain of corpses — images conjured by Davinite priests who look into the warp, displaying the Legion's allegiance to the powers Horus has embraced. Targost, a lodge captain and as much holy man as commander, delivers a prayer of victory in the guttural tongue of Davin, and the Legion chants it back before marching.

Loken and Torgaddon, crossing the plaza towards the basilica, run into World Eaters — and Loken finds himself facing Khârn. For a moment he sees the noble warrior from the Museum of Conquest, and then something else swamps it. He asks what they turned him into. Khârn answers: "I am the Eightfold Path," and says there is no way off the Path, that they must always go further. Loken sees his soul and despairs — the warrior who swore the same oaths he did, drowned in bloodshed and betrayals yet to come. They grapple in the mud until a Sons of Horus Land Raider crests the rubble and its dozer blade punches through Khârn's chest, carrying him off impaled while Loken presses into the mud beneath the tracks.

In the palace, Tarvitz cannot understand how the traitors got in until he realises the breach came through Lucius's sector and nobody has seen Lucius. He sends Solathen's warriors back in good order and goes east alone to find him. Noticing that Eidolon's assault is so fixated on the prize of the palace that it has left the outer wings uncovered, he sees an opportunity, and orders Solathen to disengage, hand the fighting over to the Luna Wolves, rally as many warriors as he can and converge on his position.

Then Lucius steps out into a dome filled with the bodies of loyalist Emperor's Children — none shot, all carved apart by an edged weapon — cutting a fresh scar into his own cheek with a shard of glass. Tarvitz asks who the latest scar is for. Lucius says: it's for you, Saul. He admits the betrayal freely and says he would do it again. Asked why, he blames his "misplaced acquaintance" with Tarvitz for putting him down here at all, and argues that the Emperor made the primarchs by genetic manipulation, so Fabius's work is only the next link in that chain — they are a superior race and must establish superiority over lesser beings, even their own. He says he is going to rejoin his Legion, the loyalists here tried to stop him, and Tarvitz will too.

The duel is one-sided; Lucius is the pre-eminent blade master and lands cut after cut without killing, playing with him. But Tarvitz is buying time. When he sees movement at the dome's edge, he throws down his sword and charges bare-handed — a move so suicidal that Lucius is a fraction too slow — and they brawl on the ground where skill with a blade is irrelevant. Tarvitz quotes back what Loken taught Lucius the first time he beat him: understand your foe and do whatever is necessary to bring him down. Then he rolls clear and shouts for Solathen to fire. Lucius is hit repeatedly but dives through a shell hole in the wall and vanishes. Tarvitz smashes his fist into the wall in frustration, thanks Solathen for his life, picks up a fallen bolter and tells them to go and show the traitors how the real Emperor's Children fight.

At the Mackaran Basilica, Loken and Torgaddon cross a forum that is a desert of ashen bone, where thousands of civilians gathered hoping the parliament house would take them in and died there. Loken feels a terrible sense of doom on the steps and wishes he had fitting words for Torgaddon, who understands and says: yes, I know, but what choice do we have? Inside, standing on the central stage before the lectern, are Ezekyle Abaddon and Horus Aximand.

## 17 · Winning is survival, the Dies Irae, and the end

Loken calls Abaddon betrayer; Abaddon says there was nothing to betray. He tells Loken he envies him, because to him the galaxy must seem so simple — as long as there is someone he can call enemy, he will fight to the death and think he is right. He insists he does not enjoy killing his brothers and that he believes as surely as Loken does: there are powers in the galaxy the Emperor does not understand, and if he leaves humanity to wither in his quest for godhood, those powers will swamp them and every human being will die. Torgaddon tells him he is a fool, that Erebus has lied to them all and that they have made a pact with evil powers. Aximand answers that those powers saved the Warmaster's life, that Horus can control them, and that the Warmaster will be lord of the warp as well as the Imperium. Loken says the Warmaster has been corrupted, and that if he takes the throne it will not be humanity that rules — and that Aximand knows it, even if Abaddon does not, because Abaddon does not care about the galaxy, he just wants to be on the winning side. Abaddon does not deny it: winning is survival. You die, you lose, and nothing you believed ever meant anything. Asked what his own mistake is, he says talking too much, and attacks.

Torgaddon and Aximand fight, and then both lower their guards, because there is no need for posturing between men who have fought together so long. Aximand says that if this could have ended another way they would have taken it, and offers Torgaddon a way out — the Warmaster wants Loken dead but said nothing about him. Torgaddon refuses, and tells him they called him Little Horus because he looked so like Horus, but that they were wrong: Horus never had that doubt in his eyes. Maybe Aximand is on the wrong side, and maybe this is his last chance to end his life as a Space Marine rather than a slave. Aximand says he has seen the warp and cannot stand against it; Torgaddon says, and yet here I am. Aximand says it will be just like the practice cages; Torgaddon says it is nothing like that.

Loken, badly beaten, lures the slower Terminator-armoured Abaddon beneath a sagging portico and cuts the pillar, bringing tonnes of marble statuary down on him. He fights through the dust to the central stage — and finds Torgaddon on his knees, limbs shattered, with Aximand's sword raised. He screams for him to stop, and hears Aximand's words with terrible clarity: "I'm sorry." Then the sword comes down.

Loken's rage turns on Abaddon, who is dragging himself from the rubble but pinned from the waist down. He leaps on him, pins his arm and forces his chainsword down through Abaddon's breastplate towards the bone shield over his heart, and holds it true. Abaddon says there is hope for him yet. Then he calls on Terminator armour's strength, surges up out of the rubble and slams his energised fist into Loken's chest, shattering Loken's own bone shield. Abaddon takes the bloodied sword from Aximand's hand, saying it killed Torgaddon and it is only fitting that he use it to kill Loken, and tells him to think about the chance he had while he dies. Then the parliament house explodes as an iron foot the width of the building smashes through the wall: the Dies Irae, striding through the ruins of the Choral City. Loken smiles as the building collapses on him, and the world goes black.

Tarvitz, meanwhile, has led a counter-attack that turns Eidolon's assault into a near-rout, but Eidolon pulls back in disciplined ranks before Tarvitz can reach him. Then Vipus calls him to look west, and Tarvitz sees the Dies Irae. He tells Vipus not to waste effort on the Titan and to kill enemy Space Marines instead. The Titan's plasma bolt takes out a whole corner of the Warsingers' Temple, killing hundreds. Tarvitz, blinded and deafened, understands that the traitors are simply waiting for the temple to fall so they can walk in and kill the loyalists at leisure, and that this is not just a battle but the Warmaster demonstrating his superiority. He shouts defiance he does not feel — "You'll never kill us all!" — sees the effect it has on his warriors, and remembers that he has a duty to make their last moments mean something.

Abaddon drags himself and Aximand out of the wreckage and reports to the Warmaster that it is over. He expected savage joy and finds only an emptiness in his soul like a vessel that can never be filled. Aximand asks what they have done; Abaddon says what needed to be done, that the Warmaster ordered it and they obeyed. When Aximand says they were their brothers and starts weeping, Abaddon tells him they were traitors and that is an end to it — and privately decides that Horus Aximand will need watching.

In the strategium, Horus contemplates Isstvan V, where Fulgrim is preparing the way, and reflects that Isstvan III was the prologue. Maloghurst reports Angron has returned last, as expected, and gives the butcher's bill: heavy losses in the landings and the palace, the Emperor's Children and Death Guard mauled, the World Eaters barely above half strength. He says plainly that the battle was costly and could have been shortened, and that he does not believe there was any great victory to be won. Horus answers that Maloghurst sees only the physical cost and not the psychological gains: Abaddon has been blooded, the real threats among the rebels eliminated, and the World Eaters brought to a point where they cannot turn back. Then he admits Maloghurst was right that he allowed himself to be drawn into a war he did not have time to fight — and rectifies it by ordering the city bombed off the face of the planet.

Loken lies pinned under rubble, unable to move his legs, coughing blood, watching streaks of fire fall through a crack in the debris and recognising them as an orbital bombardment — a final epitaph leaving no one in doubt who won. Fire bursts across the ruins and darkness falls.

A hundred loyalists remain in the shell of the Warsingers' Temple — Sons of Horus, Emperor's Children and a few lost-looking World Eaters, no Death Guard — surrounded by ten thousand traitors standing motionless in the ruins. Tarvitz says they want to plant a flag on their corpses. Vipus asks whether they gave them the fight of their lives; Tarvitz says they did, and that even once they are gone Garro will tell the Legions what was done here, and the Emperor will send an army bigger than anything the Crusade has ever seen. Vipus answers that he will have to. Solathen asks whether it meant anything, and Tarvitz tells him yes — they hurt them here, and they will remember it. Then the bombs begin falling on the palace, and the loyalists do not run for cover, because there is no point.

In a nearly finished temple aboard the Vengeful Spirit, beneath an altar with four faces representing what Erebus calls the four faces of the gods, the officers of the new Crusade gather over a hologram of Isstvan V and the Urgall Depression. Horus tells them seven Legions are coming to destroy them and will find them there — but that it will not be a battle at all. Erebus reports that all goes well at Signus, that Sanguinius and the Blood Angels will not trouble them, and that the Ultramarines are mustering at Calth suspecting nothing: their allies outnumber their enemies. Aximand, sunk in a strange melancholy since the Choral City, asks what happens after the trap is sprung, since the Emperor will still reign and the Imperium will still answer to him. Horus answers: then, Little Horus, they strike for Terra.

## The main themes, in short

* **Betrayal turns inward.** Book two was about Horus being deceived; this one is about what he does with that. Every betrayal here is committed against a brother — Legion against Legion, Lucius against Tarvitz, Aximand against Torgaddon, Aruken against Cassar, Turnet against his own crew.

* **Who is loyal to what.** Almost every character is forced to choose between loyalty to an institution and loyalty to the idea behind it. Garro trusts his honour brother over his own fleet. Qruze breaks with his Legion to keep the code that made it. Abaddon chooses the Legion; Loken chooses the oath.

* **Faith becomes the resistance.** The Lectitio Divinitatus starts as a forbidden cult and ends as the thing that gives Sindermann, Keeler, Cassar and eventually Qruze the nerve to act. Horus himself concludes that they underestimated what a civilian with genuine faith can do.

* **The cost of perfection.** The Emperor's Children's pursuit of perfection curdles into Fabius's laboratory, Eidolon's implanted scream, and Lucius's scars and hunger for sensation. Tarvitz's refusal of that "opportunity" is what marks him as expendable — and is exactly what makes him the man who saves everyone.

* **Small acts against enormous odds.** One captain steals a gunship, one ship's captain fires on his own side, one old warrior remembers Cthonia. None of it saves Isstvan III, but the whole rest of the war depends on it.

* **Winning is survival, versus dying for something.** Abaddon's creed — you die, you lose, and nothing you believed ever meant anything — is set directly against Tarvitz's answer to Solathen: they hurt them here, and they will remember it.

* **The prologue.** Horus reads every setback as raw material for a bigger victory. Isstvan III does not go to plan, costs him dearly, and he still calls it a gain — because his warriors are now past the point of turning back. The book ends with the trap already set at Isstvan V.
