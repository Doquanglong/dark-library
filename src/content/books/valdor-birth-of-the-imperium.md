# Synopsis

Set generations before the Great Crusade, in the years after the Unification of Terra. Constantin Valdor, first Captain-General of the Legio Custodes, is placed under formal enquiry by Uwoma Kandawire, a High Lord of Terra investigating what the Custodes did at Ararat — and what became of the Thunder Warriors who won the Emperor his world.

# Chapters

## 1 · Sevuu

Sevuu, an investigator, meets High Lord Uwoma Kandawire at a dig site in the empty highlands of eastern Anatolya (once called Urartu). She travels in a battered flyer so the Palace's spy networks won't notice her. The land is huge and dead — Terra is wrecked by centuries of chemical war and collapse.

They drive up to Mount Ararat, an ancient holy name now banned. Kandawire says a battle here was about symbolism, not force, and the real question is what kind of strength the Imperium is built on. In the trenches: battlefield debris, an armour pin with the Raptor Imperialis (an eagle's head and lightning) — an emblem she argued against, because it made them look like destroyers rather than builders. The armour fragments are mixed: unknown ceramic, plain metal, and a rare gold-like material that breaks the analysers and is still warm.

Tens of thousands died here. Sevuu calls it senseless. Kandawire orders the dig shut down in two days because the truth in the ground is dangerous, and says it wasn't senseless, isn't the end or the beginning, and there will be more.

## 2 · The Palace and Valdor

The new capital is being built high in the mountains, kept alive by oxygen processors and buried reactors. Nobody asks why He (the Emperor) chose such a mad spot — people assume He must have a reason. Decades of success have made many think He is invincible, which the book calls dangerous. Organisations exist to stamp out difficult ideas, mostly by book bans and quiet warnings. Terra is not really united, and the most dangerous enemy is belief — which the Emperor says He came to end.

The Custodian Samonas watches the half-built Palace (Tower of Hegemon, Senatorum Imperialis) and thinks it is not fated or divine, just ordered and built. Foundations sit on older foundations.

He reports to Constantin Valdor, captain-general, recently pulled from the wars and in a poor mood. Valdor calls it "a ludicrous place for a city" and says the Custodians are the real symbols. Asked about "her" (Kandawire), Samonas advises killing her now because she knows. Valdor refuses: knowledge is not a crime, and this is meant to be an age of law, not a warlord's instincts. He orders Samonas to gather evidence and find a weakness. He also says the Emperor does not truly trust anyone — the stronger you become, the weaker everything around you is. Sanction: "use your judgement."

## 3 · Kandawire's first interview

Kandawire's view of the same city: high, clean, never conquered by any warlord — the right place for the seat of Unity, even if it's currently a freezing building site with cold showers.

Her aide Armina warns her not to insult Valdor; he could kill her with a flick of his fingers. Kandawire answers that there is more than one kind of strength, and that the law binds them — "that's what makes this Emperor worth serving. At least, that is what we must believe."

Her backstory: born to a modestly wealthy family in the Banda Confederacy, south-east Afrik, beside a dried-up ocean. She learned the world from an old atlas projected on a wall. Her father taught her the line she lives by: the warrior is the servant of the worker — when the warrior rules, things stop working. Her mother died of radiation-caused cancer; her father died when raiders (zooipa) overran the compound. Ophar rescued her, and she refused to leave until he saved her projector — she never went back for her father, and it still shames her.

At the Tower of Hegemon she meets Valdor face to face and, despite her fear, formally places him under enquiry under the Lex Pacifica. He agrees at once and asks what she wants to know.

## 4 · Ophar's spying / "Maulland Sen"

Ophar, old and gangly, moves around the Palace disguised as a senior Administratum official with forged passes and a fake augmetic eyepiece. He notes how quickly people have relearned obedience.

He has been secretly photographing the Inner Walls and counting Custodians. He then buys information from a black-market dealer calling himself Voranchek. The data-slate shows large shipments of advanced small arms — boltguns, grenades, incendiaries — coming into the Palace under seals so high nothing has been skimmed, probably issued near the top of the Senatorum. Ophar threatens Voranchek with what he knows about him, and leaves. Voranchek asks what anyone could possibly need all that for. Ophar: "I don't know. That's the problem."

Back with Valdor, Kandawire sets up her recorder and asks her first real question: Maulland Sen. Valdor answers: "That is not a name I recall with any fondness."

## 5 · Transcript: Maulland Sen

Valdor's account, 126 years ago. Maulland Sen was a frozen confederacy in the far north, ruled by the self-styled Priest-King — a kingdom of witches. The Emperor had marked it out early; the goal was not just land but removing sorcery from Terra. Valdor insists sorcery was real, whatever people now believe.

Forces: the Fourth Legio Cataegis (Thunder Warriors), cognomen "Iron Lords," under the primarch Ushotan; twenty-two regiments of regular troops; thirty Custodians as protection. The campaign was a test of whether a whole Legio could work at scale.

Conditions were murderous — equipment froze, unenhanced troops lost their minds, ghosts followed them in the wind. Valdor states plainly that Terra could not have been conquered without the genecrafted troops. Over seven thousand Thunder Warriors died in the final assault.

Ushotan broke into the citadel first and slaughtered everyone inside, including slaves who might have been saved. Valdor calls this their greatest weakness: powerful but unstable, hard to control once loosed. Afterwards Ushotan, laughing and bloodied, said he finally understood why he had been made — and that Valdor would never feel that, and he pitied him for it. Valdor was troubled: he felt they had unleashed something hard to keep in bounds. The transcript cuts off as Kandawire presses him on where that feeling led.

## 6 · The decision, and Liora Harrad

Kandawire and Armina discuss the interview; Valdor has granted another audience. Kandawire is surprised he wants to talk, and says he talks "like a man without pride."

Ophar reports two things: (1) the Custodians have pulled off the normal patrol routes and are gathering underground beneath the Senatorum, and their numbers in the Palace are very small — most are fighting elsewhere; a new regiment, the Castellan Exemplars, is filling the gap. (2) Large illegal shipments of advanced weapons have been waved through and are somewhere in the Palace.

Kandawire says she can still call everything back, then gives the order: begin.

Second thread: Liora Harrad, a technician in the "Dungeon," a vast secret laboratory city buried under the mountains where thousands work on gene-samples in amniotic vials. She slips away to a storage depot to check a manifest — the last batch. The crates are gone, and she smells incense. Samonas is waiting for her.

## 7 · Achilla, the High Lords, and the second interview

Achilla, an old augmetic mercenary, waits in the wastes with a huge gathered army. His history: he fought for money in the old anarchic days and enjoyed it, then the Emperor's Imperium swallowed the little kingdoms and made soldiering into a profession. He once watched a Custodian kill from three kilometres away and found that level of power obscene and unfair — monsters dressed in gold. Drifting to the margins, he ended up with malcontents and true believers. A signal reaches his tracker: [Proceed]. Thousands of engines start.

Background section: in the beginning there was only the Emperor, then Malcador (whose real power is deflection, lies and uncertainty), then Valdor — made from Terran stock, the template of the Legio Custodes, dour and reserved. As conquest grew, government was needed: the Lex Pacifica, and four High Lords Civilian. Kandawire got the post of Provost Marshal, master of the law and the Arbites. Years earlier she told Malcador that when the wars end, the victorious army turns on the people, and only the civilian power and the law shields them.

Second interview, during a huge storm. Kandawire asks about a rumour of a secret programme of new generals for the armies. Valdor answers flatly: there are no new generals — but he knows the incident that started the rumour, and will tell it.

## 8 · Transcript: the disaster in the vaults

Twenty-six years ago. Valdor was meeting Malcador and the genewright Amar Astarte about the Thunder Warriors' incurable instability — warriors whose blood, organs or muscle would suddenly turn on them and kill them. They knew it was coming, which made them reckless, and by then they risked becoming an empire within an empire. Malcador wanted to push genecraft further; Valdor did not share that faith, and thought the Custodians plus the huge unenhanced armies were enough for the next phase.

The meeting was cut short by alarms from a distant secret facility. Valdor gathered his Legio and they tore the skies apart to get there, with Malcador and Astarte alongside. The place was collapsing, bodies everywhere — technicians, workers, even Custodians dead inside intact armour. The Emperor was there but invisible, holding the structure together. The same sickness Valdor tasted at Maulland Sen was there many times stronger, and he understood that the Priest-King had only ever been a splinter of something far greater. The greatest chamber was already lost: twenty vessels emptied, lightning still snapping between them.

Astarte insisted on going deeper to save the secondary repositories — walls of gene-vials fifty metres high running back into the dark. Valdor pressed his whole order into carrying caches out, and carried vials from the flames himself, feeling life pulse in the glass. Much was saved, much was abandoned. His point to Kandawire is that the programme was meant to cure the instability and produce the leaders the new armies would need, and it failed: there are no new generals. She then asks the real question — the Thunder Legion has gone silent for months, and she has been to Ararat and seen the remains. What happened there? Valdor: "You are out of time."

## 9 · The convoy climbs; Kandawire runs

Achilla's army grinds up the mountain roads through sleet and thinning air, past their own broken-down transports. They drive straight through an industrial town without a shot being fired — no walls, no towers, and the residents barely look up, because the world is at peace and this is just another regiment heading for the plateau. His friend Slak says the trouble was that everything changed too fast. Achilla gets one glimpse of the Lion's Gate through the blizzard, feels a strange warning in it, then shrugs it off: it's a doorway, and doorways get kicked in.

At the Palace, Kandawire returns to a fully mobilised complex. She tells Armina that Valdor did it — she looked into his eyes when she asked and found no evasion and no guilt, and she believes there is nothing he would shrink from if he thought it served his own interests. She sends Ophar away, ordering him to run.

Before boarding, she gives Armina a ciphered recording device locked to Armina's blood — both interviews plus the Ararat evidence — and tells her to keep it safe. She quotes a saying from home: when the elephants fight, the grass gets trampled, and they are the grass. "We are unimportant, but this must survive." Armina realises she does not expect to come back. The convoy of armoured groundcars roars out into the storm.

## 10 · Valdor arms; Astarte kills Ilaed

Valdor is ceremonially armoured in auramite by a dozen serfs. The ornamentation was the Emperor's order, not his taste — Malcador told him at the start that he is the bringer of the new age and the warden of the old, the destroyer and the preserver, and over time the Custodians came to treat their armour as part of themselves. Valdor reflects that he has never had an arrogant thought since his ascension, only faint snuffed-out memories of what jealousy or rage felt like, and wonders how much he lost to gain what he has. He takes up the Apollonian Spear, once carried by the Emperor Himself.

Samonas reports over the link: Kandawire's forces are supplied and will strike within the hour, and he has detained the technician Liora Harrad, who knew little but enough. Valdor gives him full sanction to act, but refuses to join him — there are multiple storms tonight, and Samonas must put this one out himself.

Meanwhile Magister Ilaed goes to report Harrad's disappearance to Amar Astarte, the greatest mortal genewright alive, who could have made herself flawless and instead let the horror of her work show on her skin. She muses that Valdor would have let everything burn if ordered, because none of his kind can disobey, and calls that the weakness at the heart of the empire. She says the new creations are ready and as perfect as they will ever be, then tells Ilaed that Harrad was acting on her orders — which makes her a traitor too — and shoots him dead. She voxes her captain-at-arms, Jelice Bordamo of the Castellan Exemplars: mobilise, move on the Senatorum, they strike tonight.

## 11 · Ushotan revealed; Ophar realises

Kandawire's convoy smashes out of the Palace through an unfinished section of undermined wall while the outer city slides into panic. Outside, an army is waiting: mercenaries, augmetic regiments and cast-offs, but mostly Imperial regulars whose loyalty to the founding ideals has not yet been worn away — gathering them in secret was the hardest part of the whole plan and the thing she is proudest of.

At the command ridge she meets the Thunder Warriors: battered bronze armour patched with hammered steel, badly maintained servos, the stink of rotting flesh, some already fighting their pre-combat mania. Their master is Ushotan, the last Thunder Legion primarch, still just about in control and still just about sane. Kandawire reminds him that this is a restoration, not a destruction — no more bloodshed than needed. He answers that they never broke their oaths and are not about to start.

Ophar's reasoning is then laid out. He was convinced long before she was: dress killers in the armour of gods and they will be worshipped while they raise their blades. The Thunder Warriors were the beloved face of the crusade, and Valdor erased them at Ararat without reference to the High Council — the act of a petty warlord, not an enlightened state. Because the Legion was never abolished by statute, it is technically still lawful Imperial force, and that is the legal ground the whole enterprise stands on. Ophar climbs to his old watchpost expecting the Custodians to finally come out and man the walls. They never do. Instead the Senatorum erupts in explosions and troopers in dark blue — Castellan Exemplars — are moving inwards, not outwards. He realises: we have this all wrong.

## 12 · Bordamo's attack; Valdor walks out alone

Captain Bordamo has no will of his own. The Castellan Exemplars were gene-tinkered from conception in hundreds of secret clinics, all since destroyed. They are baseline humans, only healthier and sharper — a contingency, built loyal not to the Emperor but to the Prophet, who is in a real sense their mother. Every time they salute and say "For Him," each of them silently says "For Her." The order they have waited their whole lives for finally comes, and they turn on the Palace from inside, killing guards and setting off charges to scatter the defenders into a hundred false emergencies.

Bordamo's drop-cage jams in the shaft — a single Custodian is hauling it back up by the chain, hand over hand, while sixteen lasrifles fire into him at point-blank range and do nothing. Samonas throws the cage across the muster-hall, kills the squad, and asks Bordamo where Astarte is. Bordamo says she is already in place, and Samonas crushes his throat. He blames the Order's own caution — they waited too long, always too cautious — and flings himself down the burning shafts toward the deepest repositories.

Outside, Valdor walks alone through the storm and out through the Lion's Gate, ordering it sealed behind him: none shall leave by it, none shall enter. He meets the enemy embassy in the open ground between the armies — Ushotan, two dozen Thunder Warriors, and Kandawire. She formally demands he surrender the city and stand trial. He refuses, and tells her the Lex is only a tool for controlling the psychologically free, an expression of the Emperor's will and nothing more; she was a fool to think it was more than that. Ushotan says he would like to go out with his blade in Valdor's spine. Kandawire presses her last point — the Custodians are too few, and Valdor told her himself there are no new armies. Valdor corrects her: he said there were no new generals. He kindles the spear, and out in the storm ten thousand helm-lenses ignite and begin to advance.

## 13 · The Prophet

Astarte named herself the Prophet long ago as a small private joke, because the Emperor was so prim about the old religious tropes, and it amused her to be the antithesis of what they were building. For a long time it was nothing more than that, and the Exemplars were only an intellectually satisfying contingency, a fief within a fief, kept in case of a future she never really expected.

Her history: the Emperor found her in a life of brutal obscurity, where she had survived by making augmented soldiers for warlords — monsters that led pain-racked lives and often died screaming on the slab. She still hears them in her nightmares. Under the Emperor she rose fast, given laboratories and then the inner circle, tasked not with the painstaking Custodians but with armies that could be mass-produced. The Thunder Warriors were the best that could be made under those conditions and were never meant to be permanent; their replacements were to be stable, disciplined, numerous and lasting. The Emperor once called them her progeny, her legacy, and in moments of weakness she let herself think of them as hers.

Then came the night the vaults were breached and the twenty primal subjects destroyed — the anchor that was to keep the new Legions from repeating the old instability. Her heart broke silently, and she heard words in those vaults that only she should have known. She believes it should have been stopped that night, but nobody was ever strong enough to argue against Him, and their own pride will not let them turn back now. She judges Valdor as a sentinel who only ever watches and waits, a weakness stamped into his essence, and she knows secrets about him — his original name, his birthplace, what the Emperor risked to find him — possibly more of his early life than he knows himself. She cannot touch the Custodes; they existed before her. But everything else carries her marks, and if a sample is corrupted beyond hope, there is only one course of action.

## 14 · The Angels; Samonas and Astarte

Achilla is on the ridge, furious that the whole army is being held back for one golden figure walking out alone to talk. Then his targeter starts twitching madly and shows a whole new mass of signals advancing in formation from the north, and the reading dissolves into static. The charge is finally ordered. Slak is killed instantly by a single explosive round. Achilla meets one of the new warriors: huge, dark grey plate hammered flat and functional, a winged blade icon, laying down impossibly accurate fire — but its movements are slightly hesitant and raw, and he realises the thing is still learning how to kill. He lands one good hit on exposed cabling, tells it to watch its cabling next time, and is killed.

Deep below the Palace, Samonas cuts through Exemplar after Exemplar down through burning laboratory decks, where the destruction has been done thoroughly enough to make sure the work can never be revived. He reaches an enormous vault — a shaft hundreds of metres long, its walls glassy with rows of vials marked by gold sigils: a raptor, a lightning strike, a winged blood-drop, a wolf's head — already strung with cables and incendiaries.

Astarte is on a rising platform behind a personal void shield, and she is not trying to escape. She tells him it cannot be completed and cannot be made perfect: they are making Thunder Warriors again, the originals are gone, and only the dregs remain. She says the Emperor believes He is running out of time and is terrified of it, and will go to war with a poisoned army of flawed monsters because He never listens. Samonas throws his blade into the one gap in the platform's rail, blows the void shields, leaps up and crushes the detonator from her hand — but the useless detonator was the oldest trick in the book. "Strike the warrior, fool, never the weapon." The real trigger is wired into her forearm, and even as he severs her arm the signal goes out and the incendiaries fire.

## 15 · Valdor kills Ushotan; Samonas survives

The battle outside is already effectively won, and the air still tastes of ashes to Valdor. The Thunder Warriors never retreat an inch. They must know at some level that this is the end of their short epoch, and they fight anyway, determined to leave the stage as they entered it. Ushotan mocks the newcomers — they fight like they're constipated, just like Valdor. Valdor calls them the Angels of Death and admits they are raw: still bleeding from their implants, minds bruised by imperfect conditioning, weapons only just locked to their helms, and full of points of potential failure. He also admits he does not much like them either. But acting together they are already terrifying.

The two of them fight. Ushotan says they always knew something was coming — He never made them stupid — and warns that one day the Angels will realise the truth about Valdor and see him coming for them too, because everything He made for these wars is dispensable except Valdor himself. Valdor answers that he is nothing, an instrument to be cast aside when its function is performed. He finds the gap, drives the spear through the primarch's breast-plate and slams him down, snapping his spine.

Kneeling with a knife for the mercy stroke, Valdor says he takes no pleasure in it and that Ushotan was one of their finest commanders. Ushotan repeats what he said at Maulland Sen — that he pities him, and means it. He lived, short and painful as it was, and would rather that than Valdor's way: no joy, no hate, no fear, unbreakable without growth, immortal without passion. He asks what more the Emperor could take from Valdor that He hasn't already. Valdor has a sudden vision of a far-future galaxy burning, with himself still in it, unchanged and feeling nothing but responsibility. He kills him, then answers softly: "Nothing. Nothing at all." Meanwhile Samonas falls through the exploding vault, shatters both legs on the floor, and drags himself to the threshold as the structure collapses behind him. His brothers pull him clear. He asks about the vaults and is told: destroyed, nothing recovered. He mutters that there will be a price for this. There is always a price.

## 16 · Kandawire and Valdor in the ruins

Kandawire ran the moment she saw the new warriors, because one look was enough to know they had walked into a trap. Thinking back, she should have listened to her own doubts: Valdor is no fool, the Emperor is no fool, and the Palace's vulnerability had always been bait dangled in front of them. She is not built for running, falls repeatedly, and expects to die at any moment.

Instead, the grey warriors keep passing her by. She finally kneels in front of one and screams at him to do it, that killing is all he knows how to do — and he simply looks at her, then turns away contemptuously and goes after other prey. With Ushotan's men they are pitiless; with her, nothing. She notices what makes them different from the Cataegis: the Thunder Warriors were warlords, gaudy and headstrong, while these are line troops, undifferentiated and collectively devastating.

Valdor comes for her, limping slightly, armour dented and jewels smashed. He tells her to go and make a life for herself — "You were a High Lord." She accuses him of toying with her from the start; he says no, she could have chosen to trust. She insists she wasn't wrong: the Senatorum and the Lex are a front and these are the real faces of the Imperium. Valdor tells her the truth she was never given — the Emperor's ambition goes beyond Terra, ships are already being built, Luna is next, and gene-scientists there can turn thousands into hundreds of thousands. For now the task is survival, and if that needs a few gentle myths of choice and self-determination, so be it. She warns him that a myth spins out of control, and that things let loose now cannot be reined in later. She asks how they differ from any other tyrant except in power; he removes his helm and answers that they are a necessity, standing between ignorance and annihilation, and that to prevent the second they enforce the first. She asks one last time about Ararat and he refuses: truth is only worth something if it is preserved, and Ushotan's name will be forgotten within a lifetime. She calls him a coward. He helps her to her feet, says he desires nothing, least of all power or vengeance, and lets her walk away. Asked whether he will always be with them, he says he expects so — he doesn't really know where else he would go.

## 17 · Malcador's secret

Malcador returns from Luna, where the Selenar gene-cults have made themselves mad and emissaries will be needed for the next stage. He asks how the Legion performed; Valdor says well enough, with weaknesses, and that recruitment and training remain taxing. Malcador wants three detachments rather than one, and complains that the Emperor always insists on haste, seeing only the objective and never the trajectory — which could get them into trouble one day. Valdor reports Kandawire permanently removed from office. Both admit they liked her, which Malcador finds funny coming from Valdor.

Then the first reveal: the destruction of the repositories was allowed to happen. Copies had already been quietly lifted to Luna, and the loss had to look real so that the many eyes watching them would relax their gaze and leave the remaining caches safe. Astarte was given every chance and chose not to pull back. Valdor objects, with a note of reproach, that he had to conceal the truth from his own kind, and that the lies used to be left to Malcador's people; Malcador tells him to get used to it, because more will be necessary. Astarte's name stays on the project documents — a mild irony the Emperor apparently enjoys — and the Dungeon can now be repurposed.

Then the second reveal: the Emperor believes the primarchs were not annihilated after all. He cast His mind on the ether and found echoes, and thinks they were scattered rather than destroyed, perhaps by an enemy constrained by rules of its own, since they are said to enjoy games. Valdor argues that if they were taken, they will carry their captors' touch, and asks whether it can be wisdom to seek them out at all — Astarte, who knew them best, thought the Legions so dangerous without them that she tried to destroy everything. Malcador answers that they never got this far without risk, and warns that the Emperor is determined; He has begun calling them His "sons," though His human sentiment is still ebbing. Valdor's parting thought is that the cause is literally all he has, and he can only hope the same will be true of these sons. Finally, in the medicae, Samonas wakes expecting censure and gets the opposite: no blame attaches, because his real role was never to stop Astarte reaching the Dungeon but to stop her destruction going any further, and he did that with exemplary efficiency. He is forbidden to ask further questions. Valdor then sets him a new task drawn from Malcador's judgement that their enemies play games — generate threats and answer them, let danger in close for the knowledge it brings, war games with the Palace itself as the prize, the contest to first blood. He ends with the Dungeon's new purpose, which will one day make it the most secure location in the galaxy: "The Imperium is born, the Palace is secured. All it needs now, I believe, is a Throne."

## 18 · Epilogue — Armina and Kandawire

Armina gets off-world, deciding Terra will soon have no corner free of watching eyes. She slips away in a skimmer from Kandawire's own vehicle pool during the confusion, spends months and a lot of coin arranging illegal passage, and ends up strapped into a nervous hauler lifting from a shabby orbital port toward a rogue trader's ship, the *Arquebus*, captained by one Alphoise de Ketasta-Phoel. Once Terra falls away beneath her, she finally plays Kandawire's recording for the first time and hears a voice she has never heard before: "Maulland Sen. That is not a name I recall with any fondness." She keeps listening.

Kandawire walks across the plateau for days, is taken in and fed by ordinary people in the mountain settlements, then keeps moving as Arbites patrols increase and the population's shock turns to a wary fear that the bad old days are not so far behind. Nobody recognises her; her environment suit soon makes her look like any vagrant. She finds her old accounts still active, which tells her Valdor never intended her to starve. She crosses back into Afrik and returns to her ruined childhood compound, roofless and empty, the town abandoned even by scavengers.

She makes a life there anyway — repairing the old water purifier, finding tinned food hidden under a mattress, keeping a fire going to hold off the wild dogs, trading with passing caravans for seeds and dry goods. Weeks later Ophar walks down the path, having escaped as she told him to. They sit on the veranda. She says she messed it up; he says she was right about most things — there are soldiers everywhere now and nobody pretends the regime is different any more. What she cannot understand is why Valdor wanted to talk at all, when he was ahead of them the entire time and never needed to. Ophar suggests he was probably lonely. She says she doesn't think they get lonely. He says they're the loneliest people on the planet. He also tells her everything she planted is going to die. She says they'll start building a roof in the morning.

# Feelings
