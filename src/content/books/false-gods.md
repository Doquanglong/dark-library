# Synopsis

The Great Crusade that has taken humanity into the stars continues. The Emperor of mankind has handed the reins of command to his favoured son, the Warmaster Horus. Yet all is not well in the armies of the Imperium. Horus is still battling against the jealousy and resentment of his brother primarchs and, when he is injured in combat on the planet Davin, he must also battle his inner daemons. With all the temptations that Chaos has to offer, can the weakened Horus resist?

# Chapters
## 1 · Petronella of House Carpinus, the Dies Irae, and landfall on Davin

Petronella Vivar, Palatina Majoria of House Carpinus, sits at her writing desk aboard a ship newly arrived at Davin, going over the roll of primarch names — Magnus, Rogal Dorn, Leman Russ, Corax, Night Haunter, Angron — and above them all, Horus, the Warmaster, whom his men call Lupercal. She has come from Terra to become his documentarist, carrying a letter written in the hand of the Sigillite himself. She is scornful of the remembrancers already with the fleet: Sindermann's texts irritate her, Karkasy's poems paint the Crusade unflatteringly, though she grudgingly admits Euphrati Keeler's pict images are wonderful. Her mute bodyguard Maggard, gold-armoured with augmetic eyes and a Kirlian rapier, answers her summons; his life is forfeit if anything happens to her. Her mnemo-quill, which picks up surface thoughts, catches what he thinks of her as he leaves.

The story cuts to the Imperator-class Titan Dies Irae of the Legio Mortis, being readied in a hangar. Moderati Primus Jonah Aruken arrives hungover and needles his fellow moderati, Titus Cassar, about calling the Titan "she." Aruken catches Cassar hiding a Lectitio Divinitatus chapbook and warns him that Princeps Esau Turnet would make him a gunnery servitor if he found it. Cassar argues that Horus's authority comes from the Emperor and that even the Warmaster must answer to the God-Emperor. Aruken tells him the Emperor himself denies being a god; Cassar answers that only the truly divine deny their divinity. Aruken then goes up through the Titan's leg to the command deck and has the adepts link him in, the cortical-dendrites sliding through the corners of his eyes into his brain.

On the surface of Davin, an old woman named Akshub watches the drop pods come down. She has waited for this day since warriors from the sky told her, when she was a baby, of the sacred task appointed to her. Thirty drop pods land at 16:04 zulu, three hundred warriors form a perimeter, and a Thunderhawk sets down escorted by Stormbirds. Ten Sons of Horus march out, and behind them comes Horus himself, in sea-green and gold with a purple cloak and a red eye on his breastplate. He looks at the world and says he never thought he would see this place again.

## 2 · Loken in the practice cage, Maloghurst's errand, and the summons to Davin

Mersadie Oliton watches Loken train in the practice cage and is disturbed by how obsessive it has become; a blade cuts his arm, the first time she has ever seen him wounded there, and he is out of breath, which for an Astartes astonishes her. They talk about age — Loken says none of the Astartes has ever lived long enough to find out whether they grow old — and about whether the Great Crusade will ever end. Loken says that since joining the Mournival he has spoken to people who think the Great Unification will never be finished, or won't last. Abaddon interrupts, grips Loken's arm and asks whether the sparring machine really drew Astartes blood, telling him he is getting soft and spends too much time with poets and scriveners. When Abaddon leaves, Mersadie sees something new in Loken's eyes as he watches him: wariness.

Loken remembers Davin from sixty years before, when it was Sixty-Three Eight, the eighth world brought to compliance by the 63rd Expedition. The feral tribes had fought bravely, surrendered honourably, and embraced the Imperial way, so Horus let them stay and left Kor Phaeron of the Word Bearers to finish the work. It had been a good war, won quickly and bloodlessly. Loken also remembers the Legion's renaming — Luna Wolves to Sons of Horus, white plate repainted sea-green — and how he had repainted his armour himself, and how a flicker of unease had passed through him at the new name, which he told Torgaddon carried "a whiff of self aggrandisement."

Hours before planetfall, Maloghurst carries a data-slate into the Warmaster's sanctum, meaning to leave it there rather than deliver the bad news himself. Horus is sitting in the dark, fully armoured, head in his hands. He tells his equerry, "I don't know anymore, Mal," and asks whether Maloghurst trusts him. The message is Petronella Vivar's demand for access, backed by the Sigillite. Maloghurst calls the demand ridiculous; Horus asks whether he is so insignificant that he does not require remembrance. When Maloghurst calls him a god among men, immortal, Horus explodes: surely the Emperor would not have made a being able to grasp the infinite and let it exist only for a short span. He says Maloghurst is right, and Erebus too, and that ten thousand years from now he wants his name known across the heavens. He grants Petronella her audience, and asks Maloghurst whether he will follow him no matter what occurs. Maloghurst swears he will follow until the galaxy burns and the stars go out.

On the ground, the expedition finds no war at all — only heat and quiet. Horus says he can tell by the smell that there is no fighting here, and that they are here because they have been *summoned*, a word Loken does not like. A column of Word Bearers Rhinos rolls across the steppe towards them, and Loken spits the name: Erebus. Meanwhile Ignace Karkasy, brought down at Loken's invitation, sits bored in a yurt full of dignitaries — Lord Commander Hektor Varvarus, Maloghurst, Princeps Turnet, the Mechanicum adept Regulus — listening to the Davinite lodge master Tsi Rekh chant. Euphrati Keeler is beside him, changed since the Whisperheads, wearing a chain with something hidden at the end of it. When Erebus enters, Keeler tells Karkasy who he is, and Karkasy suddenly understands why Loken wanted him there. Loken had asked him to come and listen to someone he does not trust.

## 3 · A sheet of glass, a man of fine character, and Temba named a traitor

The day before planetfall, Loken finds Kyril Sindermann deep in Archive Chamber Three, surrounded by old texts full of snarling monsters and burning men, and returns *The Chronicles of Ursh*. Loken presses him: the passage about Anult Keyser's last battle, in which a lieutenant named Wilhym Mardol became a daemon and killed his own commander, sounds far too much like what happened to Xavyer Jubal in the Whisperheads. Sindermann deflects, but the conversation turns to how thin civilisation is. He tells Loken that the wall between civilisation and barbarism is not steel but "a thread, a sheet of glass" — a touch here, a push there, and pagan superstition and the worship of fell beings come back. He insists he is not in despair, and that thanks to the Emperor mankind is close to a golden age. He adds that he dreads to think what might become of them if the Emperor were ever taken away. Loken tells him that will never happen.

At the War Council, Horus has briefed the Mournival on a plan, but Erebus derails it. Loken reflects on how much has changed since Xenobia: the Warmaster is sullen and argumentative, withdrawn into his sanctum with only Erebus for counsel, and the Mournival feel their exclusion keenly. Horus is also plagued by administrators from Terra, chiefly the high administratrix Aenid Rathbone and her eaxectors demanding the Emperor's Tithe from newly compliant worlds. Horus once told Loken he would kill every eaxector in the Imperium if he could — and meant it.

Karkasy, pushing through the crowd with Keeler behind him, is overwhelmed by his first sight of Horus. He remembers his job in time to watch Erebus, and sees the Mournival greet the First Chaplain like a brother — with a little reticence from Loken — and sees something silver, a coin or medal, pass between Abaddon's palm and Erebus's. Then Erebus interrupts the Warmaster in front of everyone to correct him: Davin is not compliant. He suggests the matter might be better discussed privately, having just raised it in a room full of people. He reveals that Eugan Temba, the Imperial commander Horus personally chose and left behind, is a traitor on Davin's moon, that the moon's tribes used what the old texts would call sorcery to twist his men, and that Temba names Horus the lackey of a fallen god. Horus, enraged, swears he will tear Temba's head off. Erebus suggests the matter be left to those beneath him; Horus refuses, orders a speartip readied, and declares that he will lead it himself.

Afterwards Loken finds Karkasy writing a poem about Temba, and asks what he made of it. Karkasy lays out his reading: the whole thing was a performance. Erebus offered privacy only after making the accusation public. He said Temba had turned against *Horus*, not the Emperor — he made it personal. He counselled against Horus going, knowing full well the Warmaster could never back down. Karkasy's conclusion is that Erebus wanted the Warmaster's humours unbalanced and his choler up, and that Erebus wants Horus to go to Davin's moon. Abaddon appears at the yurt entrance, calls Karkasy a worm, says he should kill him for the dishonour done to Erebus, and tells Loken his dalliance with wordsmiths has turned his head. As the First Captain turns to leave, Karkasy asks him one more question: what was the silver coin he gave Erebus?

## 4 · Secrets and hidden things, no such thing as Chaos, and Petronella's audience

Abaddon freezes, then lunges; Loken gets between them and sends Karkasy running. Loken argues him down by pointing out what would follow if an Astartes murdered a remembrancer in cold blood. Then he presses Abaddon on the coin. Abaddon says three times that he cannot say, then, chest to chest with Loken, admits the truth about the warrior lodge: they did not bring Erebus in. Erebus brought *them* in.

Loken cannot stop the word "liar" surfacing, and remembers Euphrati Keeler asking him whether, if he saw the rot, he would step out of his regimented life and stand against it. He remembers a conversation with Erebus on the forward observation deck two weeks earlier. Loken had asked about the warp, and about Chaos — the word Mithras Tull of the interex used on Xenobia, describing it as a distinct force, the source of the most malevolent corruption imaginable, which would outlive them all and dance on their ashes. Erebus hesitated, then insisted the warp is nothing but mindless energy, that *The Chronicles of Ursh* is the work of taletellers, and that Tull was a xenos-fraterniser whose word cannot be trusted. He promised Loken flatly that there is no such thing as Chaos. Loken said nothing, but found to his own astonishment that he believed the enemy over the brother Astartes — and noticed that nobody had ever said exactly what kind of weapon was stolen from the interex Hall of Devices.

In the Retreat, a bar aboard ship, Karkasy drinks and complains to a remembrancer named Wenduin about Petronella Vivar's connections. He remembers Loken finding him shaking after the confrontation and making him swear an oath of moment on a page torn from his Bondsman Number 7 notebook — an oath never to tell another living soul what passed between them. Euphrati Keeler arrives, transformed, dressed in a plain remembrancer's robe with her hair cut short, looking for Loken. After she leaves, Karkasy finds a folded pamphlet on his table marked with an "I" with a haloed star at its centre, teaching that the Emperor is God and God is the Emperor. He gathers up every copy in the bar, tells Wenduin to find some other fool to seduce, and goes to Keeler's billet, where the same symbol is etched beside the door. He holds up the pamphlets and says: *Lectitio Divinitatus* — we need to talk. She was expecting him.

Petronella waits in Horus's surprisingly modest chambers and remembers her first audience. Horus had refused her offered hand, told her not to call him "my lord," asked bluntly why she wanted the role, and cut her short when she spoke of glory and fury, asking whether she had ever seen a battle. He told her it is only those who have never fired a shot who cry aloud for blood. Stung, she told him that no one else in his ragtag band of remembrancers would do as good a job, and that if he thought he could bully her he could go to hell — "sir." Horus laughed and said he liked her, that he saw the fighter in her, that there is something of the wolf in her. Now, in the present, he catches her looking at an old book his father gave him, showing astrological diagrams and a creature part man, part horse. He puts it away. She senses fury bubbling under his calm. He tells her he cannot talk today because of matters on Davin's moon, then offers to take her to the embarkation deck to see how the Astartes prepare for war.

## 5 · Our people, the Warmaster's oath of moment, and the speartip lands

The Vengeful Spirit prepares for what everyone is calling not subjugation or pacification but *extermination*. Boas Comnenus, Master of the Fleet, moves the ships the short distance to Davin's moon, which looks to him like a bloated pustule against the heavens. On the embarkation deck, five companies of Astartes, Varvarus's Byzant Janizars and a Legio Mortis battle group including the Dies Irae are readied against perhaps ten thousand rebel Army soldiers. Loken raises the thing that troubles him: these are not xenos or a lost strand of humanity, they are *our people*. Abaddon answers that they are traitors and their lives are forfeit. Loken presses further — what if it happens again, world after world; what if *Astartes* rebel? The others laugh at him, Aximand pretends to accuse him of sedition, and Torgaddon breaks first. The four of them stack their hands: kill for the living, kill for the dead, to hell with both — kill for the Warmaster.

Horus brings Petronella down to see it. He tells her about Ullanor, six hundred vessels, two million Army soldiers, a hundred Titans, and how only he and Abaddon walked away from the fight with the greenskin warlord, whom he "illuminated" and threw from the highest tower. She asks whether it is unusual for one of such rank to fight in person. Horus says that for men to follow him without question they must see him sharing the danger. She asks what would happen if he fell, and he says twice, flatly, that he will not. Loken and Abaddon argue about the remembrancer programme; Abaddon says they are not here to write history but to make it, and Loken answers that the record is not for them but for the generations to come, who will look back and understand the nobility of what they set out to do.

Horus introduces the Mournival to Petronella one by one and tells her that without them the office of Warmaster would be a poor thing indeed, that in them the humours of choler, phlegm, melancholia and sanguinity are mixed exactly as he needs to keep him on the side of the angels. Then he kneels before them, holds out a wax seal and a strip of parchment, and asks his sons to hear his oath of moment. Stunned, Loken reads it out: does Horus swear to take vengeance on those who defy him, to leave none alive who stand against the future of humanity, and to do honour to the XVI Legion? Horus grips Abaddon's blade barehanded, swears on the weapon, and seals the oath paper in his own blood. The deck erupts in chants of "Lupercal!"

The Stormbirds launch. Amid the vast tide of drop-ships and Mechanicum landers, one small gold-skinned skiff leaves the civilian bay unnoticed. On the way down, Vipus makes Loken switch to channel seven, where a wet, gargling voice repeats a message: it renounces the ways of the Emperor and his lackey the Warmaster of its own free will, promises that if he dares come here he will die, and that in death he shall live forever — "Blessed be the hand of Nurgh-leth." Loken is reminded of Samus and the Whisperheads. The flight officer confirms it is a real vox signal, from a ship-sized transmitter that has no business being on a planet's surface, and that it only began once they hit the ionosphere. Loken warns Horus it may be a trap; Horus dismisses it as propaganda and orders the speartip to lock onto the signal and destroy it. They land in stinking bog and yellow fog, on ground that should by the briefing texts be hot, dry forest. Loken tells Torgaddon that something bad happened here. Something very bad.

## 6 · Land of decay, the dead things rise, and the Glory of Terra

The Astartes advance through the fog in four phalanxes, one behind each member of the Mournival, with Horus at the front, unhelmeted, and the Imperial Army struggling behind in rebreathers. Armour bogs down in the marsh, but three Mechanicum landers come down and the Legio Mortis Titans step out: the Dies Irae leading the Death's Head and Xestor's Sword. Loken watches with unease, wondering why the Warmaster thought such engines necessary. He examines a tree and finds it is not dead but diseased and full of maggots. Then his boot cracks something in the water — a skull — and hundreds of bodies float up, disturbed by the Titans' footfalls. He confirms from a shoulder patch that they are men of the 63rd Expedition, Temba's men. As he says so, the corpse he is holding grabs his throat, its eyes lit with green fire.

The whole swamp erupts. Corpses and bloated one-eyed, horned things rise and attack; clouds of flies rot an Astartes alive when he tears his helmet off. Loken shouts to aim for the head, since the dead things get up again otherwise. Torgaddon is dragged under by a huge cyclopean creature and has to gut it with a combat knife. Loken is pulled down into the water and only freed when Vipus and Luc Sedirae haul him out. Meanwhile Petronella, who took Horus's glint of mischief for permission and followed the invasion down in her gold-skinned skiff, is shot out of the sky by the Dies Irae, whose crew could not identify her craft. Maggard drags her from the burning wreck and holds off the horde alone, killing with his pistol until it is empty and then with sword and dagger; the Kirlian rapier drops anything it touches instantly. He is bleeding, blistered and greying when the Sons of Horus break through.

Horus lifts Petronella to her feet and demands to know what she is doing there, telling her that if her bodyguard had been less capable she would be dead. He questions Maggard, learns from Petronella that House Carpinus chaperones removed the man's vocal cords so a bodyguard would not speak in his mistress's presence, and frowns at that. He asks about the blade — Petronella explains it is Kirlian, forged on ancient Terra, said to sever the connection between soul and body. Horus makes the sign of the aquila, tells Maggard to be proud, and refuses to let him kneel. Maggard offers the Warmaster his sword hilt first. Petronella sees a new and frightening devotion in her bodyguard: *I am yours to command.*

The four phalanxes regroup at the crash site. The Byzant Janizars have been badly mauled and will not advance, even under their discipline masters' electro-scythes. Erebus staggers in with his depleted Word Bearers and reports that he has found the source of the transmissions — a structure a kilometre west. Horus tells his warriors they have been grossly wronged and it is time to avenge their dead. They march, the ground firms, and then, as suddenly as stepping from one room into another, they are out of the fog. Ahead of them rears an iron mountain: Eugan Temba's flagship, the Glory of Terra.

## 7 · Watch our backs, the ship collapses, and the Betrayer

The ship has lain broken on the mudflats for nearly six decades, keel snapped, spires fallen, hull hung with vines. Abaddon says nothing could have survived the crash, so Temba must already be dead; Horus asks who then is broadcasting. Loken points out that the signal only began when they entered the atmosphere, so someone activated it. Erebus urges them to go inside and kill whoever is there. Loken objects, and Erebus sneers at whether the Sons of Horus are now afraid of the dark; the Mournival close ranks behind Loken and Erebus backs down with an apology. Horus decides: they are going in.

Loken makes one more attempt on the march, asking whether Horus truly values the Mournival's counsel or only uses them as war dogs. Horus promises to listen, and Loken says plainly that the Warmaster should not be leading the speartip and that they should not enter without reconnaissance — at least let the Titans soften the target first. Horus tells him he has a thinker's head, but wars are won by men of action, and grows briefly and genuinely angry when Loken says he need not bear his honour alone. Then he laughs it off and says: mark my words, everything achieved in this Crusade so far will pale beside what he has yet to do. He does accept the Titan bombardment. Torgaddon, unusually serious, tells Loken he thinks this is a trap and that they are being funnelled inside; Loken's answer is that they watch their backs.

Horus divides the force. Sedirae's assaulters take the vanguard, Verulam Moy and his flame and melta units accompany the Warmaster, Erebus and the Word Bearers go round the far side to catch Temba between them, Abaddon and Aximand form echelons left and right — and Torgaddon and Loken are left outside to secure the rear and the line of withdrawal. Loken is stunned to be left behind, and Torgaddon says plainly that Moy is a good file officer but not the man who should be at the Warmaster's side at a time like this. Inside, vox contact degrades to nonsense. Moy tells Horus he thinks Loken was right, that delving into the unknown like this is not heroism but guesswork. Horus answers that the whole art of war is guessing what is on the other side of the hill, snaps that he is sick of being told what he can and cannot do, and orders them on. Then Sedirae's voice cuts through: *They're here!*

The whole ship tears free of the mud and topples, slamming down with the force of an orbital strike. Loken is thrown into a pool, dozens of his warriors are buried, and the shockwave finally blows away the fog — revealing hundreds of the dead things marching towards them. Inside, Horus pulls an iron spar out of his own chest and finds himself alone, his gorget vox smashed. He recovers his golden sword, forged for him by his brother Ferrus Manus of the Iron Hands to mark his investiture as Warmaster, and works his way up towards the bridge, noting that a lung has been torn and simply switching his breathing pattern. He fights through a shuffling phalanx of horned, one-eyed famine victims, cutting down hundreds, until they abruptly stop being solid and dissolve into black-green smoke, and he recognises the ship for what it is: a spawning ground of the immaterium.

On the bridge, the workstations have been ripped out and rewired into the vox transmitter, and dead men of the 63rd Expedition are stitched into the hanging banners. Horus whispers that they will be avenged. Then he sees the vast, corpulent figure standing before the captain's throne, flies in every fold of its skin, gold epaulettes still on the rotting cloth, one hand in an infected wound in its own chest and the other holding a sword with a glitter-sheen like diamond. And at its feet lies Verulam Moy, his neck broken. Horus knows the killer before he looks up: Eugan Temba, the Betrayer.

## 8 · Fallen god

Outside, Loken's warriors run out of ammunition — something he can barely remember happening before — and fight on with blades. The dead things gain the rubble slopes three times and are thrown back three times, and each time a handful of Astartes are dragged under. With the Warmaster's fate unknown, the Astartes are brittle and fight badly.

On the bridge, Temba greets Horus as Warmaster and Horus refuses him the right to use the title. Temba insists he really is Temba — the loyal follower left to rot on a backwater while Horus went on to glory. He says Moy would have made a fine convert, that he offered him a place and Moy refused and tried to kill him, and that his loyalty was admirable if misplaced. He tells Horus the warp is beautiful, that it seethes with life and ambition, that it offers the power to dominate and control. Horus says the galaxy already has a master, called the Emperor. Temba answers that the Emperor blundered across the cosmos destroying anyone who would not submit and then left Horus to pick up the pieces — a tyrant by another name. He names the underlying destiny of the galaxy: Chaos. Chaos was the first power in the universe and will be the last, and so long as Man endures, so does Chaos.

They fight. Horus runs his sword through Temba's chest and the wound does not trouble him. Temba's own blade moves with impossible speed, and Horus realises he is not fighting Temba's skill but the sword itself, which has a hunger of its own. He sees a screaming man flickering inside the bloated flesh. Temba's anklebones snap under his own weight and Horus feels a flicker of pity, whispering that he should have listened to him. Finally both blades land at once: Horus's through Temba's heart and lungs, Temba's into the muscle of Horus's exposed shoulder. Horus severs Temba's arm, and the sword clangs to the deck still twitching in the dead fingers.

With the warp light gone from his eyes, Temba weeps and asks what he has done. Horus kneels beside him and tells him it was the warp, that the tribes used it against him. Temba refuses the excuse: they showed him what it could do, he saw beyond and into the warp, he saw the powers that dwell there, and he still said yes. He tells Horus there is great evil in the warp and that he must know the truth of Chaos, and gives him a vision: the galaxy a wasteland, the Emperor dead, mankind in bondage to a nightmare of bureaucracy and superstition — all grim darkness and all war. Only Horus has the power to stop that future, and he must be strong. Then he dies. Horus severs the transmitter cables with one blow, sits down beside the body, and weeps for his lost friend.

Outside, the dead things suddenly all drop at once. Vipus asks why they stopped when they had won; Loken has no answer, and privately wonders what will happen when the Astartes meet a foe with a will as implacable as their own. Abaddon and Aximand come out of the wreck battered, then Erebus, then Sedirae's men are dug out of a lower deck after an hour — Sedirae haunted, with seven dead. The Mournival go in together and find Horus sitting on the darkened bridge cradling Temba's swollen head, with a puncture in his breastplate and a bleeding stab wound in his shoulder. He says he failed them all, that he should have listened. Abaddon pulls Loken aside: they must get him out and no one can be allowed to see him like this, because seeing the Warmaster humbled would break the Legion. Horus insists on walking out himself, and tells them to gather up Verulam.

Petronella watches the Mournival come down with Moy's body on their shoulders, grim and silent, the Warmaster behind them. Loken tells her to be silent and be gone; Horus overrules him, saying that if being his documentarist is to mean anything she must see them at their worst as well as their best. He tells her Moy fell in the line of duty. Asked whether Temba is dead, he says Eugan Temba died a long time ago and he does not know exactly what he killed in there, but it was not him. He says he has no trouble with his enemies — he can take care of enemies in a fight — it is his so-called allies who keep him walking the floors at night. She asks what Temba said, and Horus begins to answer that only he had the power to stop the future, then his eyes roll back and he collapses. Her mnemo-quill writes what is in her head: *I was there the day that Horus fell.*

## 9 · Silver towers, a bloody return, and the veil grows thin

On Prospero, in the City of Light, Magnus the Red stands on the balcony of the Pyramid of Photep, his eye aching from a nightmare in which he saw the Athenaeum ablaze. He has been cursed and blessed with foresight all his life. He is still bitter about the Council of Nikaea, where the Emperor forced him to renounce sorcery, and thinks his father's doctrine of science and reason is a comforting blanket thrown over a humanity kept ignorant of the real powers in the galaxy. In his visions he has seen a corrupt chamber, the glitter-sheen of a sword, and the blow that will change the fate of the galaxy — death and betrayal, loyalty tested, terrible fates for his brothers, and a father entirely ignorant of the doom coming. Ahriman, his Chief Librarian, comes to ask whether he has decided. Magnus tells him to gather the coven in the catacombs. Ahriman warns that the spell is very dangerous and asks why they would use sorcery to warn the Emperor when he has forbidden it; Magnus answers that when his father sees that sorcery saved his realm he will not be able to deny that it is vital. The Emperor's palace is warded against the warp, and only a conjuration of such power will breach those wards. He tells Ahriman to gather them but wait, because Horus may yet surprise them.

On Davin's moon, the Mournival are frozen by Horus's fall, and it is Petronella — a mortal woman — who reacts first, kneeling in the mud and begging him to get up. Shamed into motion, Loken calls for an Apothecary and treats it as a battlefield injury, ordering the armour off and pressing his ear to the Warmaster's chest to find the hearts beating in a deathly slow double beat. The Apothecaries take over, saying the Larraman cells are ineffective and the problem is hypoxic poisoning. Abaddon weeps openly. Aximand tells Loken he has faith the Warmaster is too strong and too stubborn to be kept down. A Stormbird takes them all up to the Vengeful Spirit.

Aboard ship, Karkasy tells Mersadie about his falling out with Euphrati Keeler over the Lectitio Divinitatus. She had urged him to pray to the God-Emperor and said the Emperor's light had healed her after the Whisperheads; he had answered that ignorance and fear create gods, that enthusiasm and deceit adorn them and human weakness worships them, and she threw him out. Mersadie agrees they should tell someone — probably Sindermann. Then alarms sound and the ship fills with weeping crowds: word is spreading that the Warmaster is dead. Karkasy and Mersadie watch from an observation gantry as the Stormbird lands. They see the Mournival carry a shrouded body out, then the gurney, and Karkasy realises with relief that the Warmaster is alive because the Apothecaries are still working on him. Then the blast doors open and the crowd surges in, and the Astartes club their way through, trampling and killing to reach the medical deck. Karkasy watches the bodies below and says over and over: how dare they. Then he sees Euphrati Keeler moving among the injured, handing out Lectitio Divinitatus pamphlets, and she is not alone.

Maloghurst replays the pict of the embarkation deck again and again with Hektor Varvarus at his shoulder. At least twenty-one are dead and many more will not wake. Maloghurst says he has shut down non-command vox traffic and is rounding up witnesses; Varvarus tells him nothing stays secret, that he must be honest, and that people will demand blood. When Maloghurst says he cannot give up an Astartes, Varvarus reminds him of the oath they both swore, and says the Imperium must stand for something or it stands for nothing — Astartes warriors have committed murder. Before Maloghurst can answer, Ing Mae Sing, the blind Mistress of Astropathy, arrives to report that the astropathic choirs are unsettled: they sense a powerful and growing surge in the warp. The veil between worlds grows thin.

## 10 · Apothecarion, prayers, and the Warmaster's confession

Vaddon and Logaan fight to keep Horus alive. The chest wound has practically healed itself, the lung sealing off while it repairs, but the shoulder wound will not clot and is bleeding him out. Abaddon, half mad with fear, slams Vaddon against the wall and swears to kill him if the Warmaster dies. Vaddon tells the Mournival the truth: they are like children trying to repair a logic engine dropped from orbit, because only the Emperor really understands a primarch's body. He describes what the poison does — it is absorbed by the red blood cells in preference to oxygen, inducing anaemic hypoxia, carried faster because of the Warmaster's accelerated metabolism. It has exactly the right genetic camouflage to fool his defences. It is, Vaddon says, a primarch killer, pure and simple. Asked how to stop it, he says that if they could retrieve the blade that made the wound, perhaps something could be done. Loken swears to find it, and Torgaddon says he is coming too.

On Davin's moon, waiting for the Titan recovery, Titus Cassar takes out his Lectitio Divinitatus chapbook and prays for the Warmaster, holding a pendant he had a servitor make for him — a silver "I" with a golden starburst. Jonah Aruken and a group of the Titan's crew come up behind him, and instead of the expected mockery Cassar sees grief and a need for comfort. He opens the book and tells them to pray.

Petronella is summoned to the medical deck. Riots have broken out across the fleet as word of the Warmaster's death spreads faster than Maloghurst's denials; doomsayers proclaim the end times and Army units are quashing them faster than they can appear. Vaddon warns her that Horus fades in and out of lucidity and that she leaves when he says so. Horus is grey and sunken, and asks whether he looks that bad, telling her not to lie because she is to hear his valediction. She protests, and he reminds her that she said her vocation was to immortalise him.

What follows is the Warmaster's confession. He blames himself for Temba, who begged not to be left behind and said it was too much for him. He says Guilliman and the Lion will laugh, and will say he was not fit to be Warmaster because he could not read the hearts of men. He says his brothers were jealous when the Emperor named him — Angron especially, and Guilliman, who thought it should have been him — and that only Lorgar, Mortarion, Sanguinius, Fulgrim and Dorn were gracious and meant it. He says he was not made Warmaster because he was most worthy, only because he most embodied the Emperor's need at that time: for the first three decades of the Crusade he fought at the Emperor's side and alone felt the full weight of his ambition to rule the galaxy. Asked whom he would have chosen instead, he answers Sanguinius, who alone has the Emperor's soul in his blood and holds all of it — vision, strength and the wisdom to rule after victory. What Horus carries of his father is the ambition to rule.

Then the bitterness comes out. The War Council is gone and he takes orders from the Council of Terra. Petty functionaries and administrators have supplanted him; eaxectors, scribes and scriveners demand to know the cost of everything. He asks what becomes of the warriors who conquered the galaxy — are they to be gaolers and peacekeepers? He says he did not dream himself into the man he is; he hammered and forged himself on the anvil of battle, and now everything he won will be handed to weak men and women who never shed blood in the dark places of the galaxy. Petronella is shocked, and at the same time her ambitious core realises she has the makings of the most sensational remembrance imaginable, one that will destroy forever the myth of the Crusade as a united band of brothers. Horus apologises for shattering her illusions, then asks whether she likes it, whether it is what she came for. She admits it is, and asks if he will tell her everything. He says he will.

## 11 · Answers, a devil's bargain, and the anathame

Loken prepares a Thunderhawk to return to the moon, ashamed now that he has time to think about the crowd they battered through — remembering the crack of skulls, and Sindermann's line about civil behaviour being a thin veneer over the animal at the core of man. Hundreds keep a candlelit vigil outside the blast doors with votive papers. Tybalt Marr, called "the Either" because of his likeness to Verulam Moy, "the Or," asks to come; the two had been inseparable, and Marr wants to see where his friend died. Loken takes him.

Aximand watches Abaddon destroy a third sparring servitor in half an hour. Abaddon rages that the Emperor would not let this happen, that he has not been with them since Ullanor, that he left them to clean up what he could not be bothered to finish for some project on Terra. Aximand warns him he is in dangerous territory and says that if he were anyone else he would strike him down; Abaddon retorts that they are pledged to the commander, and Aximand answers that their Mournival oath also pledged them to the Emperor above all primarchs. Abaddon tears a mesh panel from the cage and hurls it — and it lands at the feet of Erebus, who has been standing in the doorway "long enough." Erebus reassures them that they are all lodge members and that if asked what he heard, he would say he cannot say. Aximand feels not reassured but beholden, as though the silence were a bargaining chip.

At the lodge meeting in the aft hold — Targost, Sedirae, Ekaddon, Kibre, and Maloghurst, who admits he has neglected his duties as a member — Erebus makes his proposal. The Apothecaries cannot cure the Warmaster, only delay. He explains that the Sons of Horus lodge takes its structure and practices from the warrior lodges of Davin, and that during the compliance he studied them looking for corruption and instead found hope. They must take the Warmaster to the Temple of the Serpent Lodge on Davin, whose priests are skilled in the mystic arts of healing. Aximand objects that this sounds like sorcery. Erebus turns on him: would he let the Warmaster die just so they can feel pure? Targost says they do this for the Legion and there is no other choice. Maloghurst explains that the lodge must be in accord, so if Aximand refuses they go no further — but the Warmaster will die. Aximand asks about Torgaddon and Loken; Ekaddon says Loken is not one of them and turned his back on the order, and there is no time to find Tarik. Aximand realises he never had a choice from the moment he walked in. He knows it is a devil's bargain and that there will be consequences, but he will not be remembered as the warrior who stood by. He gives his consent.

On the moon, the mists and diseased forests are gone, as though Temba's death broke the cycle of decay. Loken, Torgaddon, Vipus and Marr work back through the drying, dying growths to the bridge. Marr insists on searching Temba's body himself. Behind the captain's throne Loken finds a polished wooden casket with strange symbols carved along it, empty inside, padded with red velvet. Then his men find Temba's severed arm still gripping a glittering sword with a blade like grey flint. Loken forbids anyone to touch it, prises the dead fingers loose himself, and lowers it into the casket — feeling, as he says Tarik's name, a sense of the monstrous harm the weapon could do. Torgaddon asks how Temba could have got such a thing, saying it does not even look human-made. Loken, recognising the script at last, tells him it is kinebrach: this is the anathame stolen from the Hall of Devices on Xenobia.

Aboard the Vengeful Spirit, the Warmaster is carried through the ship on a bier of kite shields, in ceremonial white armour with a silver laurel wreath, borne by Abaddon, Aximand, Sedirae, Targost, Kibre and Ekaddon, with Varvarus and Maloghurst behind. Heralds announce the route, people throw tearstained scraps of paper instead of flowers, censers of healing herbs smoke along the way and a band plays the Legion March. Aximand is awed by the grief, understanding that to these mortals the Warmaster is a symbol of the new galaxy being forged out of the Age of Strife. He decides he made the right choice. He remembers Horus telling him, right before they jumped into the greenskin city on Ullanor, that faith means knowing one of two things will happen when you step off into the dark: there will be something solid to stand on, or you will be taught to fly.

## 12 · Agitprop, brothers in suspicion, and the Delphos gate

Karkasy writes as he never has before, pouring the massacre on the embarkation deck into verse, deciding that everything he wrote before this was prologue and that the truth is what matters. The problem is circulation: the remembrancers' presswork is censored and would never pass his new poetry. Then he smooths out one of Keeler's Lectitio Divinitatus pamphlets, sees it was run off on a cheap bulk-printer, and realises that if Euphrati can get the use of one, so can he.

On the moon, Marr burns Temba's body. Leaving with the anathame in its casket, Loken watches the Legio Mortis carriers rise on pillars of fire and feels like the loneliest man in the galaxy. Torgaddon presses him on how the weapon got there, and when Loken tries to deflect, spells it out himself: only someone in the Expedition could have brought it, it had to be here before they arrived, and only one person was with them on Xenobia and could have reached Davin first. Loken agrees, but says what he cannot work out is *why* — why steal it and bring it here. Torgaddon asks whether he thinks this is a coup, another primarch making a play for the office of Warmaster. Loken says it sounds too far-fetched, like something out of Sindermann's books. Then Vipus calls them aboard: they are taking the Warmaster to Davin.

Flying down, Loken sees hundreds of landing craft descending and a long column of tanks, trucks and civilian vehicles crammed with people all heading north into the mountains. Nobody answers his hails, not even Maloghurst. He thinks through what he knows — Karkasy's reading that Erebus wanted Horus on the moon, plus Erebus's certain hand in the theft of the anathame — and concludes that Erebus wanted Horus killed here, though that still does not explain why anyone would go to such lengths. The Thunderhawk reaches a vast crater holding an octagonal stone building with eight burning towers around a central dome, ringed by thousands of Davinite torchbearers. Loken lands and marches up the serpent-lined stairs, noticing properly for the first time how far the Davinites have diverged from the human genome, and wondering how they escaped extermination when it was the Word Bearers who made first contact.

He arrives just as the great bronze gates swing shut, carved with a tree whose three roots feed a pool, twin snakes coiled through its branches — an image close to the Apothecaries' symbol. Seven warriors stand before the gate: Abaddon, Aximand, Targost, Sedirae, Ekaddon, Kibre and Maloghurst. Loken asks what they have done. Aximand tells him: the Warmaster was dying and Vaddon could not save him, so they brought him to the Delphos, the Temple of the Serpent Lodge. Targost says that by the end Horus could not even speak, that he talked to the remembrancer woman for hours before losing consciousness, and that they had to put him in a stasis field to get him there. Loken calls it a house of false gods and quotes the Emperor: civilisation will only achieve perfection when the last stone of the last church falls upon the last priest. Targost answers that with the Warmaster on the threshold of death, neither religion nor its denial seems very significant, and that if the Emperor were here he would do the same.

Maloghurst explains the rule of the place: once the Delphos Gate is shut it can only be opened from the inside. If it is the man's destiny to live he may open it himself; if not, it opens after nine days and his remains are burned and cast into the pool. Loken demands to know who told them about this place, and reads the answer in their faces: Erebus. Targost confirms it, and says that if the Warmaster lives, Loken will be thankful he spoke of it. Abaddon calls on Torgaddon to stand with them; Torgaddon says Garvi may be a starch-arse but he is right, and turns away too. Abaddon shouts after them that they swore to be true to the Mournival to the end of their lives and are now oath-breakers — a word that stops Loken in his tracks. Aximand points at the pool, where the crescent of Davin's moon is reflected, the same mark branded on Loken's helmet at their Mournival oath, and calls it a good omen. Loken shrugs him off: the Mournival oath was pantomime, but this is ritual, this is sorcery, and he owns only the empirical clarity of Imperial Truth. He tells them he believes they will all rue the day they brought the Warmaster here.


## 13 · Who are you? The ritual in the temple, and an old friend

Horus wakes naked on a beautiful hillside under a blue sky and concludes calmly that he is dead. He cannot remember anything except his name. But the world keeps flickering: mountains become towers of brass and iron, the river runs with molten slag, the grass becomes a hardpan of ash and iron. He runs, and a hundred-strong wolf pack springs out of the trees, each wolf branded with a black double-headed eagle. The lead wolf asks him again and again, "Who are you?" — and when Horus blinks he sees armour and a single staring eye behind the animal's face. The wolf tells him he must remember before *he* comes for him. Horus runs to the edge of a black crater lake with a white moon overhead, and as the wolves howl one last time it falls into place — the moon, the wolves, Lupercal, Luna Wolves — and he shouts that he is Horus of the Luna Wolves, Warmaster and regent of the Emperor, and that he is alive. He hurls himself in and the water shatters like black glass.

In a hidden chamber inside the Delphos, Erebus watches the Davinite priestess Akshub work a ritual over a circle of quicklime with eight points, a murdered acolyte at its centre. Her thralls pass around the acolyte's heart and each takes a bite, Erebus finishing it: the betrayal was offered to the Architect of Fate, the bloody feast to the Blood God, and the acolyte's defilement called on the Dark Prince and the Lord of Decay. Erebus reflects that his part in the Warmaster's downfall is one of a million events set in motion thousands of years ago, and that Davin will soon become a place of legend. Akshub warns that they must hurry because another presence is near — a one-eyed ghost who walks between worlds and seeks to return the son to his father. Erebus laughs at Magnus and says he is too far away and Horus too far gone. When he threatens Akshub she reminds him she could boil the blood in his veins with a word, and that he needs her to bring his soul back or it will drift in the void forever. Her acolyte anoints his face with crystalline antimony so that Horus will not see his true likeness but a face familiar and beloved. Then she tells him the spell needs one last thing — his death — and cuts his throat.

Horus wakes again on the same hillside, this time in his white plate, and says to hell with this, he knows he is not dead. With his identity back comes the knowledge that the world is false, and he finds that if he wills it he can see the iron nightmare behind the beauty. His last memory is of talking to Petronella in the apothecarion. He remembers a form that ghosted behind the wolf leader's face — Magnus, though surely Magnus is on Prospero after Nikaea. Then he finds an armoured Astartes floating face down in the river, turns him over, and it is Hastur Sejanus, his most favoured son, killed on Sixty-Three Nineteen.

Loken meanwhile marches away from the Delphos furious with himself for not realising Erebus had plans beyond simply murdering the Warmaster. Vipus asks whether Abaddon and Aximand are now enemies; Loken says no, they are brothers being used, as they all are. He leaves Torgaddon and Vipus to watch the temple and contact him if they see Erebus, and heads back to the Vengeful Spirit for answers.

Sejanus embraces Horus and tells him his own death was a blessing that opened his eyes to the truth of the universe: death is a country from which this traveller has returned, and "they" sent him back. He explains where they are — Horus's body lies in the Axis Mundi chamber of the Delphos, whose name in the ancient tongues means "the womb of the world," but his spirit is loose. This landscape is the warp, shaped from fragments of Horus's own will and memory; Horus recognises it as the ancient Terra of the geophysical relief map they once found buried on a dead world. Sejanus says the world is collapsing because Horus's mind and body are dying. He explains that great intelligences dwell in the warp, that there is no malice in them, only the desire to exist, that events in the galaxy are destroying their realm, and that they have chosen Horus as their emissary because they cannot exist in the material universe. If he refuses, he dies. In return for his aid they can give him a measure of their power and the means to realise every ambition. As the wolves close in again, Sejanus raises a gateway of light and quotes one of Horus's own lines back at him about towering genius disdaining the beaten path. Asked where the gate leads, he answers: to the truth.

Below, the crater fills as thousands of pilgrims abandon their vehicles and gather around the Delphos in the rain, singing songs in praise of the Warmaster by candlelight.

## 14 · The forgotten, living mythology, and the geno-vaults

Horus steps through the gate into a vast plaza on a shrine world, thronged with pilgrims who cannot see him and would forget him instantly if they could. At the centre stand statues: an outer ring of Astartes-armoured figures around a golden king holding up a torch. Horus circles them and recognises Sanguinius with his wings, Rogal Dorn, Leman Russ in wolf pelts, then Guilliman, Corax, the Lion, Ferrus Manus, Vulkan and Jaghatai Khan — and at the centre, in gold, the Emperor. A half-machine priest fused to a wheeled lectern reads aloud from a leather book while winged infants carry his voice on silver trumpets. Horus says this is a place of worship; Sejanus tells him it is an entire world given over to praising the Emperor, and that it is not the only one — there are hundreds, probably thousands. Horus protests that his father spent centuries freeing humanity from religion, that he shamed Lorgar for exactly this. Sejanus answers that the Emperor was not ready for worship then because he did not control the galaxy — that is why he needed Horus — and that he destroyed the old faiths so his own cult could more easily supplant them.

Then Horus asks the question that undoes him: where is *his* statue? Sejanus says he has walked this place many times and never seen Horus's likeness. Horus rages that he is the chosen regent, that he fought on a thousand battlefields, that the blood of his warriors is on the Emperor's hands. Sejanus tells him the Emperor has forsaken him and will soon turn his back on his people to win a place among the gods, that they were all deceived and will all be spurned, and that while they fought war after war in his name he was secretly building his power in the warp. Horus smashes the preacher off his pulpit and runs into the streets. Everywhere he goes there are prayer-whipped flagellants, gold-plated ossuaries, prayer zeppelins with loudspeakers shaped like skulls, angels carrying pilgrims into a mausoleum, death venerated in every window and carving — and nowhere any image of Horus. He shakes a devotee, shouting that the Emperor is not a god, and snaps the man's neck by accident. He sinks to his knees and cries out: father, why have you forsaken me?

Back on the Vengeful Spirit, Loken hands the anathame in its casket to Vaddon, telling him it is a kinebrach weapon forged from a sentient xeno metal, that he believes it is the source of the Warmaster's malady, and that he must tell no one he has it. Then he finds Sindermann in Archive Chamber Three, unshaven and clearly not having left in days. He asks about the Serpent Lodge. Sindermann explains that serpent mythology is probably the most widespread myth known to man, that myths travel with storytellers rather than words, and that we carry our myths with us rather than reinventing them. The serpent stands for spontaneous creative energy and for immortality, since shedding its skin made it privy to the secrets of death and rebirth; the moon, waxing and waning, was its celestial counterpart, so in old rites of initiation the moon was the goddess mother and the serpent the divine father, which is where serpent and healing become linked. Loken asks whether what is happening is a rite of initiation.

Sindermann goes further. An old Terran word for serpent, "nahash," meant "to guess" and was transliterated as "enemy," "adversary," or "Seytan" — a name they spoke of at the Whisperheads. But the Olympian root gives "drakon," the cosmic serpent that symbolised Chaos: the thing that must be overcome to create order, whose sacred years are times of great ambition and incredible risk, in which events are magnified threefold. Then he reluctantly shows Loken a passage from the Book of Atum, in which a voice declares itself Horus, forged of the Oldest Gods, he who gave way to Khaos, the great destroyer of all, who set doom in the palace of his will and follows the serpentine path. Loken demands to know what it means. Sindermann says it is a prophecy, and at its most basic level it is about the death of the universe.

Sejanus finds Horus on the steps of a basilica and tells him his body is dying and he must make his choice. The doors open on another oblong of light. Asked where they are going now, Sejanus says: to the beginning. They step through into a freezing laboratory beneath Terra, full of workers in gold-visored suits, and Sejanus points to a warded silver door. Horus feels, for the first time, fear, and says he does not want to know. Sejanus goads him — this is not the Horus he followed for two centuries — and the door opens anyway. Beyond is a chamber with a walkway and twenty cylindrical tanks, numbered and marked with the same mystical symbols, each humming with barely contained power. As Horus wipes the fog from the first hatch, something inside sees him and moves closer. Sejanus tells him these are the Emperor's most secret geno-vaults beneath the Himalayan peaks — the place where Horus was created — and Horus finds himself staring into a pair of liquid eyes that mirror his own.

## 15 · Revelations, dissent, and the scattering

Two days after the Warmaster's departure the Vengeful Spirit is a ghost ship, and Karkasy walks it distributing sheets titled *The Truth is All We Have*, three poems apiece, his favourite being *Uncaring Gods*, which compares the Astartes unfavourably with the Titans of myth. He bought a cheap bulk printer from a junkyard dealer with his card winnings; his billet now stinks of ink. In the empty Retreat he finds Petronella Vivar drunk to insensibility with three empty bottles on the table, calling herself a writer and a fraud. She tells him she came to record the glory of Horus and the splendid brotherhood of the primarchs, and that everything she thought she knew has been turned upside down. They trade insults about each other's work, and when he goads her she tells him the most incredible tale he has ever heard.

In the warp, Horus recoils from the tank. Sejanus tells him he has seen the Emperor's ultimate goal and must now see how the quest for godhood began: the primarchs were to be his generals, gods who would bestride planets and take back the galaxy for him — a weapon to be cast aside once blunted. Horus walks the line of tanks, seeing light and form indistinguishable, organisms like architecture, eyes and wheels turning in circles of fire. He stops at the one marked XI and knows its glories will never come to pass. Then Sejanus tells him there was no accident. The primarchs were not scattered by mischance: what kind of accident could reach this deep, past those wards, and set each of them down on an ancient homeworld of humanity?

On Davin, Euphrati Keeler photographs the Delphos under lightning. She met Titus Cassar the day after she arrived, following the secret Lectitio Divinitatus symbols to his prayer meeting of some sixty souls, and the congregation quickly gravitated to her sermons — Cassar has faith but is no orator, and welcomed being replaced, knowing he is a follower and not a leader. Tens of thousands have now camped in the crater over seven days, the temple steps thick with offerings, while the Legio Mortis engines stand sentinel at the valley mouth, too big to enter. As she and Cassar walk to the meeting, five Army soldiers attack them — one tells her that they know about the prayers and that Horus is the one she should be giving thanks to. They hold her down, beat Cassar and smash her picter. She claws free and reaches a dropped pistol, only to find Torgaddon has already put three of them down and is holding the last off the ground by the neck. He tears off the man's name and unit badge, promises him the Discipline Masters, and tells him to get out of his sight. Keeler salvages the memory coil from her wrecked picter. Torgaddon tells her he has been asked to bring her back to the Vengeful Spirit — by Kyril Sindermann.

Sejanus lays out the rest. Only beings of such magnitude could contemplate conquering a galaxy, and the primarchs were made for that — not for humanity, but for the Emperor. Then the accusation: could the science of man alone have created a primarch? If it could, why not a hundred, a thousand? A bargain was made. The masters of the warp, Sejanus says, are as much Horus's father as the Emperor is. The Emperor went to the powers of the warp centuries ago offering friendship and devotion, then betrayed them and stole their power for himself, investing a fraction of it in his sons. Horus whispers that they are all tainted; Sejanus says the power of the warp simply *is*, and can be mastered by a man with the will, which the Emperor lacked. And the gods struck back — not at the Emperor directly, since he was too strong, but at what he needed most.

Sirens sound. A vortex of dirty light opens beneath the ceiling and a hurricane tears at the tanks. Six Custodian Guard rush in with guardian spears, led by a man Horus recognises: Constantin Valdor, who does not know him and orders him to end the conjuration. They fire on him. Enraged that warriors of the Imperium have shot at him, Horus tears them apart — skull crushed with a fist, one split collarbone to groin, one run through, one dismembered, one beheaded — until only Valdor is left. Then the tanks start tearing free of their moorings, and Horus stops, terrified for what is inside them. Time halts and a golden giant of unimaginable majesty appears, whose voice is a perfect symphony. He looks at Horus in puzzlement and says, "I know you?" He tells Horus he would destroy his great works, and begs him to turn from this path or all will be lost. Then he weighs the consequences in a blink, turns away and lets time resume, walking off and leaving the tanks to be swept into the vortex. Horus screams after him. Hate swells in his chest, and he lets the wind take him up into the great eye of the warp with his brothers.

## 16 · The truth is all we have, the arch-prophet, and home

On the strategium, old Iacton Qruze tells Loken he knows perfectly well why they call him "the Half-heard" — not because he speaks softly but because nobody listens to a word he says. He counsels Loken not to break with Abaddon and Aximand, because when we turn from one brother we turn from them all. Then he lets two things slip. First, that Varvarus is demanding heads roll over the embarkation deck deaths, has been pressing Maloghurst daily to tell the fleet the truth and make reparations, and has already had Ing Mae Sing send communiqués about it back to the Council of Terra. Second, that some anonymous remembrancer is circulating anti-Astartes pamphlets across the ship, called something like *The Truth is All We Have*. Loken walks off the strategium without another word.

He is waiting in Karkasy's billet when the poet gets home. Loken says he vouched for him and this is how he is repaid; he calls the poems libellous trash and says he should kill him for it. Karkasy fires back: is that Astartes justice now, killing anyone who gets in the way or says something you disagree with, just like the innocents on the embarkation deck? He reminds Loken that he was the one who told him to keep telling the truth, ugly and unpalatable — the truth is all we have. He tells Loken that the Astartes demand respect but must earn it, that their ethics must be beyond question, and that they must stay not only above the line between right and wrong but clear of the grey areas between. Loken asks whether he will keep publishing, and tells him to answer honestly; Karkasy says that if he can, he will. Loken decides: if we have no truth then we are nothing, and if I stop you speaking out then I am no better than a tyrant. He will not stop him or send him back to Terra — but Karkasy has made powerful enemies, and is from now on under Loken's protection.

Keeler finds Sindermann in the sub-deck, gaunt and haggard, and braces for a lecture. Instead he asks her to teach him. He tells her that the things which dominate our imagination determine our lives and character, so we should be careful what we worship, because what we worship we become. He has been reading obsessively since the Whisperheads, trying to rationalise what happened, and has found a recurring pattern in the old texts: a being of golden light who sounds a great deal like what her pamphlet describes. He shows her the book he is struggling with, written in an unfamiliar derivation of an ancient human language, which he believes is the Book of Lorgar — possibly a copy given to the Warmaster by Lorgar himself. He reminds her of the rumours that Lorgar worshipped the Emperor as a god, and says that if his translation so far is right, the book talks about *bringing forth the essence of god*. He tells her he has spent his adult life promulgating the truth of the Imperium, but wonders whether they have been teaching the wrong message; if the thing beneath the mountains was truly a spirit of evil, then a divine Emperor is needed more than ever. Words, he says, can move the multitude, and men of words have shaped history more than generals or statesmen — and if they can prove the existence of god, the iterators will shout it from the highest towers. Keeler, staring at this arch-prophet of secular truth talking about gods, looks at the script and realises she has seen it before. She tells him to gather his notes and meet her in the archive chamber in thirty minutes.

Horus wakes on Cthonia, under polluted skies that smell of home. On the way here the powers of the warp showed him the future: xeno breeds holding whole swathes of the galaxy, the Legions shattered, bureaucrats and officialdom ruling a hellish regime where men live lives of no consequence, and a father become a carrion god who neither feels his subjects' pain nor cares for their fate. He says quietly that he will not let this happen. Then the wolves come, and the pack leader transforms — fur to copper skin, eyes running together into one golden orb, red hair, bronze armour, a cloak of feathers. It is Magnus, who tells Horus it has taken him too long to reach him, that the wards are powerful, and that a dozen of his thralls die every second to hold the way open. Sejanus emerges from the mine tunnel and warns Horus that this is a shape-changing warp creature that feasts on souls. Magnus retorts that Horus knows him, and that the impostor does not even wear his own face — then closes his fist on the air and rips the disguise away in a flare of silver light. Beneath it stands Erebus in Word Bearers plate, a long red scar healing across his throat, admitting he came in the guise of Sejanus to ease Horus's understanding, but insisting he has spoken nothing but the truth.

## 17 · Horror in the archive, angels and daemons, and the gate opens

Keeler brings her edit engine to Sindermann's table and pulls up eight picts from the War Council on Davin, all including Erebus. She zooms in on his tattooed skull, and Sindermann recognises the symbols as identical to those in his book. Using shots from different angles she builds a composite of the script wrapped around Erebus's head and prints it, giving Sindermann his grammatical key. He begins translating aloud while she wanders the stacks.

She pulls down the Book of Atum, and finds she can read it — and then realises that the words Sindermann is reading aloud are the words on her page, the letters rearranging themselves as she looks. The pages burst into flame. She runs back to find Sindermann unable to let go of the book, words pouring out of his mouth against his will, while a swirling cloud of blue light hangs above the desk and a questing limb oozes out of it. She punches him across the jaw to break the recitation, and something heavy lands wetly on the table behind them. What follows chases them through the stacks: a horror of flailing limbs and glowing flesh, lunatic faces and mouths forming and reforming across its body, hurling bolts of fire that set the shelves alight. Ing Mae Sing appears at the doorway with her arms outstretched, holding it back long enough for them to get out and shut the door.

In the warp, Erebus and Magnus argue for Horus. Magnus calls it all lies; Erebus retorts that those with the courage to speak the truth always seem sinister to the ignorant, and asks how Magnus can stand here at all except by the sorcery the Emperor forbade him. Magnus hurls fire and lightning, and none of it touches Erebus, who laughs that Magnus is too far away for his powers to reach. Horus then turns on both of them: Erebus came cloaked in another's identity, called Magnus a warp beast and then spoke to him as a brother — so if Magnus is here by sorcery, how else is Erebus here? Caught, Erebus admits the Serpent Lodge sent him, that the priestess had to cut his throat to do it and that he will kill her for it when he returns. Magnus tells Horus the future is not set — Erebus showed him one possible future, not an absolute one — and to have faith. Erebus sneers that faith is just another way of not wanting to know what is true. Horus rounds on them both, telling them he knew perfectly well this was not Sejanus, that without context everything he has been shown is meaningless, that they must take him for a fool if they thought parlour tricks would bewitch him, and that he trusts neither of them: he is Horus, and he makes his own fate.

Then Erebus plays his last card. He came at the behest of his master Lorgar, who already knows of the Emperor's quest for godhood and has sworn himself to the powers of the warp — when the Emperor rejected Lorgar's worship, other gods were willing to accept his devotion, and his power has grown tenfold. Magnus cries that Lorgar is loyal and would never turn. Horus listens and knows with utter certainty that Erebus is telling the truth, and feels disappointment, anger, and a spark of jealousy that Lorgar was chosen first — and thinks that if wise Lorgar chose such patrons, perhaps there is merit in it. Magnus, fading, begs him to be strong, saying Erebus would have him spit on his oaths, betray the Emperor and turn on his brother Astartes. Erebus asks whether it is really so terrible: deliver the Emperor to the gods of the warp and unlimited power is his, the galaxy his to rule as the new Master of Mankind. Horus roars for silence and says he has made his choice.

Outside the Delphos on the ninth day, in torrential rain and lightning, hundreds of Sons of Horus gather with Loken, Torgaddon and Vipus at the top of the steps, facing Abaddon, Aximand, Targost, Sedirae, Ekaddon and Kibre, who have kept vigil the entire time. Both sides are locked and loaded, and Loken quietly warns his men to be battle-ready while hoping it does not come to that. Targost asks whether Loken has come to fight them; Loken says no. Aximand says the bad blood must end, that to turn on one another would dishonour the Warmaster's memory, and admits the whole thing was always a forlorn hope but was all they had. Loken looks at his despair and asks himself honestly whether he would have decided differently in Aximand's place — and answers that they will wait as brothers united in hope. They cross the pool and stand shoulder to shoulder.

In the archive corridor, the horror bursts through. Keeler pulls out the silver Imperial eagle on her necklace, understanding with sudden clarity that everything since the Whisperheads has been preparing her for this. She stands firm as blue fire washes over her and it does not burn, and shouts that the power of the Emperor commands the abomination. The eagle blazes white-hot, fusing itself to her palm; Ing Mae Sing adds her powers, and the creature's laughter turns to the wails of a child as it is stripped apart. The strength streams out of Keeler with the light, and she collapses as it disintegrates, with Sindermann staring down at her in wonder.

At the Delphos, a boom that has nothing to do with the storm splits the night, the lightning stills, and the gate begins to open. Loken watches the darkness widen and his head sinks — this must be the automatic nine-day opening, and the Warmaster is dead. Then footsteps, and white and gold plate emerges from the dark. Horus strides out with his purple cloak billowing and his golden sword held high, the eye on his breastplate blazing red, more vital than ever, and says, "You are a sight for sore eyes, my sons." Torgaddon punches the air and shouts "Lupercal!", and the cry is taken up by the Mournival, the Legion, and then ten thousand pilgrims until the walls of the crater shake with it.

## 18 · Brothers, the assassination on Aureus, and this turbulent poet

Ten months on, Mersadie Oliton finds Loken repairing armour in the training halls and is struck by how much he has aged. He tells her the Brotherhood is virtually destroyed and the war will be over within the week — and that this is a war they should not be fighting. After Davin, Horus installed a new Imperial commander, Tomaz Vesalias, who like the man before him begged not to be left behind. Instead of keeping the rendezvous at Sardis, the Warmaster ordered the 203rd Fleet to muster with him at a binary cluster called Drakonis Three Eleven, telling nobody why he chose it, and no cartographer could find any record of why the place mattered. There they found human worlds — another lost branch of the old race — who greeted the fleet with joy. Three days later Angron's 203rd Expedition arrived. Six hours after that, the first shots were fired.

Loken tells Mersadie what really happened. The Auretian delegation came aboard in craft uncannily like Stormbirds, and twenty warriors of the Brotherhood marched out in silver plate that mirrored Astartes armour, carrying boltguns with no magazines fitted. Their leader, Fabricator Consul Emory Salignac, greeted Horus courteously and explained, when asked, that the resemblance was natural: the construct machines their ancestors brought from Terra sit at the heart of their society and tend towards uniformity of creation. Horus asked, with a cold edge, whether he meant STC machines. Salignac agreed and began to lower his staff toward the Warmaster — and Horus drew his pistol and blew his head apart.

Mersadie repeats the official account: the staff was an energy weapon, and when the assassination failed the Brotherhood attacked. Loken tells her to forget what she has been told. There was no weapon. The Brotherhood only tried to escape, and their guns were not even loaded. Abaddon's Justaerin cut half a dozen down before they knew what was happening, and Loken led Locasta forward and gunned down the rest as they tried to board their ship. Asked why the Warmaster would do it, Loken hesitates and then says what he saw on Horus's face afterwards: he smiled — as if the killings had been part of his plan all along. Horus, he says, *wants* this war.

Torgaddon is summoned to a lodge meeting that now fills a whole armoury with hundreds of hooded warriors; Targost tells him the lodge no longer operates under a veil of secrecy within the Legion, and keeps the ritual challenge only for tradition. Torgaddon puts a combat knife to Erebus's throat, sees the old scar there, and accuses him of stealing the anathame, of wrecking the interex negotiations, of being the reason the Warmaster was injured. Abaddon answers that the interex negotiations failed because they were *meant* to. Targost then makes a speech: the Astartes were created to kill and conquer, they are killers pure and simple, and now that the Crusade nears its end they are lambasted for it. He calls Hektor Varvarus a traitor for demanding they answer for the embarkation deck deaths. Aximand passes around copies of *The Truth is All We Have*, calling its author "this turbulent poet," and the Lectitio Divinitatus, and warns that the ship of the Imperium will sink through the mutiny of those aboard.

Then they get to the point. Maloghurst names Ignace Karkasy and says he must be silenced — and when pressed, says plainly that Karkasy must die. Torgaddon points out that the poet is under Loken's protection, and a guilty silence falls. Maloghurst explains the rest: Varvarus has taken the matter to the Council of Terra and will soon have Malcador the Sigillite's ear, and someone must be handed over. Erebus supplies the name: Loken led them onto the embarkation deck and led the way through the crowd. Aximand tells Torgaddon that only if he, Ezekyle and Aximand swear that Loken orchestrated the massacre will Varvarus accept him as guilty. Torgaddon calls it what it is — two birds with one stone, a scapegoat and a free hand to murder Karkasy — and says the Warmaster will never agree. Targost tells him it was the Warmaster's own suggestion. Torgaddon says that if betrayal and murder are needed to save the Legion, then perhaps it does not deserve to survive, and that he spits on them for thinking it. Maloghurst warns him he is either with them or against them. Torgaddon throws his silver lodge medal at Maloghurst's feet and says: then I am against you.

## 19 · Isolated, allies, and the eagle's wing

Petronella has shut herself in her stateroom for months, unwashed and unslept, transcribing the Warmaster's valediction in obsessive detail. She has realised what is missing — context. Without a framework, the words are meaningless. But no Astartes will talk to her; every request for an audience with the Warmaster is refused. Then she notices an Astartes nodding respectfully at *Maggard*, not at her, and sees his shoulder scroll marked with a green crescent moon for the Davin campaign. Her solution is to send Maggard among the Astartes to be seen and to gather what he can, reasoning that because he is mute they will assume he is simple and be less guarded around a dolt, then to read his thoughts each evening with the mnemo-quill.

In the sanctum, Horus berates Khârn, Angron's equerry, over the stalled siege of the Iron Citadel and tells him to storm it and kill everyone inside. Alone with Maloghurst and Regulus, he turns to what he actually wants. Regulus admits that recovering fully functional construct machines — the thinking engines that drove humanity into the stars — would be a prize worth any price. Horus promises him the STC machines, and then names his price: the Great Crusade is almost over but the real work is only beginning, he is poised to embark on the greatest endeavour imaginable, and he needs allies. He refuses to give details. Regulus lays a polished machine-cog on the table and pledges as much of the Mechanicum as he commands, and as much strength as he can muster from those he does not.

In the tenth month of the war a fleet translates in-system behind the Expedition in perfect attack formation, and passes through firing range without shooting. A cultured voice says that he still has a thing or two to teach Horus: it is Fulgrim. Loken, watching the pressure door open, is struck first by the great golden eagle's wing sweeping over Fulgrim's shoulder, then by a face pale to the point of albinism with eyes almost entirely pupil and shimmering white hair, and finds he has no vocabulary for such perfection. With Fulgrim come Saul Tarvitz, Eidolon, Lucius, and a new face — Chief Apothecary Fabius. Fulgrim says there have been reports that Horus and his warriors should be called to account for the brutality of this campaign, then laughs and says he has come so he can go back and tell the fops and scribes on Terra that Horus fights war the way it is meant to be fought: hard, fast and cruel. Horus answers that war is cruelty and there is no use trying to reform it — the crueller it is, the sooner it is over. Fulgrim mentions that Magnus has once again upset the Emperor and that the Wolf of Fenris has been unleashed to escort him back to Terra; Horus turns serious and suggests they talk in private. Loken sees the Warmaster smile — the same smile he wore after killing the Auretian consul — and feels a chill. In the training cages Lucius beats Loken in seconds and tells him no one will beat him in this lifetime.

The lodge meets again, this time as senior officers without robes. Only Loken, Torgaddon, Qruze and Marr are absent. Targost calls Fulgrim's arrival spying; Sedirae says the World Eaters shock even him, that they do not fight but butcher. Then Horus himself walks out of the shadows, telling them he has heard their deliberations and has come to bring new blood: Tybalt Marr, and a mortal — Maggard. Targost objects that the order is for Astartes only; Horus and Aximand answer that the only qualification is that a man be a warrior. When Maggard cannot give his name, Horus says with a smile, "He can't say." Then Horus makes his real request. He tells them the days ahead will be hard, that he is set on a course affecting every man under his command, that once they embark there is no turning back, and that there will be those in the Imperium who call them traitors and rebels whose bleatings they must ignore. He asks only whether they are with him, and they shout as one that they are. Before they act, though, they must set their own house in order: Hektor Varvarus and the remembrancer Karkasy must be silenced. Asked what he intends, Horus says: that they be illuminated as to the error of their ways.

## 20 · The breach, a midday clear, and Angron's return

The Emperor's Children leave after three days, the two primarchs having spent the whole visit behind closed doors. As Fulgrim sweeps out, Loken catches a glimpse of a horribly familiar golden sword belted at his waist. Loken has also learned that the anathame is gone: Fabius came to the medicae deck with the Warmaster's own seal and took it, and Vaddon had no choice but to hand it over. Months of research have yielded nothing and now any chance of uncovering its secrets is lost.

The Mournival is effectively finished. Abaddon and Aximand have barely spoken to Loken and Torgaddon in ten months, and none of the four has met with the Warmaster since Davin. At the Iron Citadel — a half-moon fortress of self-repairing walls set against white mountains — four months of siege have finally opened a half-kilometre breach. The order of battle puts the World Eaters in first under Angron, supported by Loken's Tenth and Torgaddon's Second, with Varvarus's Byzant Janizars following in dress greatcoats because Varvarus refuses to enter looking less than his best. Loken and Torgaddon shake hands and promise a bottle of victory wine in the ruins. Angron passes them — gladiatorial armour, chainmail cape hung with skulls, a monstrous chain-glaive, and dark iron cortex implants punched into his skull from his slave days that he has never wanted removed — and Loken sees only death in his heavy-lidded eyes. The World Eaters cut their palms and smear blood across their faceplates.

As the assault climbs and Angron reaches the crest, the ground begins to shake. The Brotherhood, who destroy what they cannot hold, detonate a seismic mine and the entire top of the breach goes up in a sheet of flame hundreds of metres high. Angron and the World Eaters are buried under a mountain. Torgaddon rises out of the rubble and charges anyway, screaming "Lupercal!", and Loken follows. They sweep the crest, and the Sons of Horus revert to the howling of the Luna Wolves. The citadel falls in less than an hour, at the cost of a primarch, hundreds of World Eaters, thousands of the Brotherhood and untold Sons of Horus. Loken and Torgaddon realise the victory is turning into a massacre and give the order to halt, and Loken sees that the Astartes have only just held themselves back from an abyss of barbarity from which they might never have climbed. He asks himself whether this world's compliance was worth it.

Varvarus arrives and the Brotherhood come out to surrender. Their leader, Senior Preceptor Ephraim Guardia, Castellan of the Iron Citadel, asks whether this is how all wars are waged in the Imperium. Varvarus answers that war is a harsh master and that excess of grief for the dead is madness, an injury to the living. Guardia calls him a tyrant and a killer, then reads out a declaration signed by all his officers: they fought the treacherous Warmaster to preserve their way of life, not for glory or riches or honour but for freedom, which no honest man could wish to give up; and rather than see their culture exterminated they surrender their citadel and their worlds — may you rule in peace more kindly than you make war.

Then the rubble bursts open and Angron erupts from it, alive, screaming "Blood for Horus!" He leaps down, cleaves the entire front rank apart, and kills Guardia with a single blow through the chest. Loken shouts for it to stop, but the surviving Brotherhood open fire and he has no choice but to order his men to shoot back. In seconds the Brotherhood are annihilated. In the aftermath Loken sees Hektor Varvarus dead in a spreading pool of blood, his body ripped open from the inside by a bolter round that detonated within him. Loken drops his sword in disgust. Torgaddon says that was no way for warriors to die, and that their deaths shame them all. Loken cannot answer.

## 21 · Illumination

Varvarus is returned to space with full honours and Horus himself gives the eulogy, praising him and promising that the Army units loyal to him will hold the world he died for so his sacrifice is never forgotten — and he keeps that promise, posting them there.

In his ink-stinking billet Karkasy is still writing, his printer working overtime on the latest edition of *The Truth is All We Have*, gathering leads on what the ship is already calling the White Mountains Massacre. There is a knock, and Maggard is standing there. The mute bodyguard hands him a folded sheet of expensive Gyptian papyrus. Karkasy frowns as he reads: they are lines from his own poems, plucked out of sequence from a dozen different works. He reaches the end and his bladder empties in terror as he understands what the note is and why its bearer has come.

Petronella, meanwhile, has found her structure — a tragedy told in reverse, opening on the primarch's deathbed with a triumphal coda about his survival — and her title, *In The Footsteps of Gods*, opening with the line her own terror gave her: *I was there the day that Horus fell*. She is impatient at Maggard's lateness when the door opens and it is not Maggard. It is Horus, in simple robes, with Abaddon closing the door behind him. He apologises for neglecting her, asks to read her work, reads it faster than any mortal could, and tells her it is very good and that she is a talented documentarist. Then, coldly: it is almost a shame that no one will ever read it.

Maggard hooks his arm around Karkasy's neck. The poet begs, weeps, scrabbles, and hears the holster stud pop and the hammer draw back. The barrel goes under his jaw and he smells the gun oil. Petronella, backing away, tries to understand; Horus tells her that when they spoke in the apothecarion he let her look inside Pandora's box, and that only one person needs to know what is in his head — given the things he has seen and done, and the things he is going to do. She offers revisions, approval over everything. She draws herself up and demands respect as the Palatina Majoria of House Carpinus, and tells him he cannot scare her. Horus says he is not trying to scare her — he is *illuminating* her — and breaks her neck with a flick of his wrist.

In a medicae cell, Mersadie and Sindermann sit weeping on either side of Euphrati Keeler, who has lain unmoving since the archive chamber, her skin like polished ceramic. The Apothecaries can find nothing wrong except the eagle-shaped burn on her hand that will not fade. Mersadie half believes Sindermann's account of the daemon and cannot decide whether he is delusional. They have just heard that Ignace Karkasy killed himself, leaving a suicide note composed in verse — which Mersadie takes as proof of his enormous conceit. Then a thin-faced man in Legio Mortis uniform knocks, with a corridor full of people behind him. He introduces himself as Titus Cassar, Moderati Primus of the Dies Irae, and says he has come to see the saint.

On the observation deck, Torgaddon shows Loken a test tube of bolt-round fragments taken out of Hektor Varvarus. They are Astartes rounds, tested by Vaddon himself, and the wound was dead centre — an aimed shot. Loken quotes a line from one of Sindermann's books about whether, in the wake of such deceit and destruction, they should despair or whether faith and honour are the spur to action. He asks Torgaddon what they are becoming, and says he no longer recognises the Legion. Torgaddon dates the change to the Technocracy; Loken says no, it was Davin — something vile and dark and evil happened to the Sons of Horus there. He says they have to uphold the truth of the Imperium of Mankind no matter what evil assails it, which Torgaddon recognises as the Mournival oath. Evil has found its way into the Legion, Loken says, and it is up to them to cut it out. Torgaddon says he is with him always, and they shake hands in the old Terran way.

In the sanctum, Horus convenes his hand-picked commanders — Aximand and Abaddon behind him, Maloghurst, Regulus, Erebus, Princeps Turnet and others — and tells them plainly that he is going to topple the Emperor from his Throne on Terra and take his place as Master of Mankind. He tells them to look at the man beside them, because in the coming fight he will be their brother, since all others will turn away: brother will fight brother and the fate of the galaxy is the prize. They will be called heretics and traitors, but they are right and the Emperor is wrong. Their one advantage is surprise. He has already contacted Leman Russ and "illuminated" him about Magnus's daemonic sorcery, and is confident Magnus will not leave Prospero alive. Fulgrim is with him and is going to win over Ferrus Manus; Lorgar too. Under the guise of the Chaplain Edict, emissaries will be sent to every Legion to seed warrior lodges. Mobilisation orders have already gone out to the Legions he cannot sway: the Ultramarines to Calth, to be attacked by Kor Phaeron of the Word Bearers, and the Blood Angels to the Signus Cluster, where Sanguinius will be mired in blood. Then a swift, decisive stroke on Terra. Asked about the rest, he says he has a plan to remove them once and for all: he will lure them into a trap from which none will escape, set the Emperor's Imperium ablaze and rise from the ashes as the new Master of Mankind. Asked where the trap will be, he names it: the Isstvan system.


## The main themes, in short

* **Pride is the way in.** Chaos does not break Horus with force. It finds the one wound already there — the fear of being forgotten, of being a tool cast aside once the fighting is done — and works on that. Every vision Erebus shows him is aimed at the same spot: *where is your statue?*

* **Loyalty to brothers versus loyalty to truth.** The Mournival oath binds the four of them to each other and to the Emperor, and the book spends its length pulling those two halves apart until Loken and Torgaddon stand on one side and Abaddon and Aximand on the other.

* **Secrecy corrodes.** The warrior lodge starts as a harmless fraternity where "I can't say" is a password, and ends as the machinery by which Erebus steers a Legion, a scapegoat is chosen, and a murder is arranged.

* **The thin sheet of glass.** Sindermann's line about civilisation being a pane of glass rather than a wall of steel is tested over and over — by Astartes clubbing civilians to death on the embarkation deck, by the massacre at the Iron Citadel, and by the Legion's own slide from crusaders to killers who take pride in killing.

* **False gods and true ones.** Everyone is worshipping something. The Imperium denies gods while treating Horus as one; the Lectitio Divinitatus makes a god of the Emperor; the Serpent Lodge offers healing from spirits; and the powers in the warp are real and are called gods by those who serve them. The one thing the Imperial Truth insists cannot exist turns out to be the thing that undoes it.

* **The truth is all we have — and it gets people killed.** Karkasy tells the truth because Loken told him to, and dies for it. Petronella records the truth because it is her vocation, and dies for it. Loken protects both and is marked for it.

* **Ends and beginnings.** The Crusade is nearly over, and nobody knows what warriors are for once there is nothing left to conquer. Horus's answer to that question is the whole plot of the book.
