# Synopsis

Gregor Eisenhorn, an Inquisitor of the Ordo Xenos, hunts the heretic Murdin Eyclone across the ice world of Hubris and finds that the trail runs much further than one man. Narrated by Eisenhorn himself, looking back, as a straightforward pursuit opens onto a conspiracy and a forbidden text that should not have survived.

# Chapters

## 1 · Tomb Point, dormant season

Hubris during the Dormant season. The planet has swung away from its sun and the ruling class sleeps out the cold in the hibernation tombs at Tomb Point, leaving the surface a frozen waste. Eisenhorn arrives there hunting Murdin Eyclone, a fugitive he has been chasing for six years. He is focused and ready, though the cold gets through his gear from the moment he lands.

He rides out to the tombs in an ice-car with his aide Lores Vibben and a small detail of custodians. The car is warm and cramped against a landscape that would kill them, and the mood inside is tense — they are closing on a dangerous man and all of them know it.

The first sign that it has gone wrong is at the tomb itself: the custodians posted to guard it are dead. That leaves Eisenhorn's party exposed. They go in carefully anyway. After six years he is finally this close, and he can barely feel his own excitement about it.

Inside, Eyclone's men are waiting, armed. The ambush turns into a close fight, and Eisenhorn kills his way through them quickly and precisely.

Then he finds Vibben on the ground, dead in the cold. It lands on him. He is known for being cold and hard-willed, and her death produces something he has no procedure for. He turns over what she had been to him — a fighter he relied on, and a companion — and struggles to place the feeling.

He puts himself back together and works the problem. He calls for reinforcements using a private code, which is the sort of thing he has in reserve without needing to think about it. He sets out where he stands in the Inquisition's factional split and why he keeps to the puritan line: the stability of the Imperium is the thing worth preserving.

The chapter closes on the promise he makes himself, which is that he will kill Eyclone.

The two halves of him sit against each other here — the hardened Inquisitor doing the work, and the man grieving underneath it. Duty against humanity. That looks like the argument the book intends to have.

## 2 · Processional Two-Twelve

Eyclone's work becomes clear: he is waking the sleepers early. Processional Two-Twelve is a vast tomb, and all through its darkened vaults the planet's elite are coming out of hibernation ahead of schedule, with none of the medical support the process needs. Eisenhorn moves through it listening to them scream in their caskets, sick and freezing and dying.

That gives him the chapter's problem. He can stop and help them, or he can go after the man who did it. He cannot do both.

Eyclone's men find him, and the fight runs through hallways littered with the injured and the dead. Eisenhorn kills several of them while Eyclone stays ahead of him, the whole pursuit conducted over the noise of people waking up into their own deaths.

At one point a sleeper begs him for help directly. He does not stop. Sacrificing one to have a chance at saving many is the arithmetic his office requires of him, and he does the arithmetic — but the chapter makes sure you watch him do it.

He catches Eyclone on a landing platform with a gun-cutter waiting to lift him out, and kills him there. It ends explosively, and it settles nothing.

Aemos and Betancore come into the story around this, and High Custodian Nissemay Carpel arrives — which means the shooting is over and the paperwork is starting.

## 3 · The reckoning at the Sun-dome

Thousands are dead, including much of the planetary nobility, and Hubris wants somebody to answer for it. Eisenhorn faces High Custodian Carpel and stands on his authority as an Inquisitor of the God-Emperor, arguing that killing Eyclone prevented something far worse than what happened. The grieving are not in the mood for the distinction.

His people work the aftermath, preserving what they need as evidence for the investigation. They carry Vibben's body back to their temporary base in the Sun-dome — an artificial environment built to keep people alive through Hubris's long dark.

He questions locals and starts pulling on Eyclone's connections, aware the whole time that he himself is being looked at, since he is the one who brought this to their world.

The local nobility say exactly that to his face when he meets them with Carpel. Eisenhorn pushes it back: their monitoring was inadequate, which is why Eyclone was able to work here unnoticed for as long as he did. It is a fair point and also a convenient one.

He gets what he wants, which is room to keep investigating, plus a local Arbites officer — Chastener Fischig — assigned to work alongside him and open up the wider network behind Eyclone.

## 4 · Thaw-view 12011

Eisenhorn and Fischig tear through the Sun-dome in a heavily armoured speeder, fast and not especially careful. The route takes them through the elite quarters, where the rich live in light and comfort and appear to have no idea what the dark outside is like.

They pull up at an expensive balcony. Fischig takes the security apart, and they breach the apartment — Thaw-view 12011.

It goes loud immediately. Gunfire meets them at the door and Fischig answers with his shotgun, which sets the tone for the rest of it. Moving up through the apartment, Eisenhorn finds a young woman hiding and badly frightened: Alizebeth Bequin, who has done nothing and is simply in the building.

He gets to Saemon Crotes and interrogates him, working toward the shape of the conspiracy behind all this. Crotes collapses mid-interrogation without warning — a reminder of how much someone is willing to spend to keep this quiet.

Then the explosion.

## 5 · Pontius

They get out of Thaw-view alive with minor injuries, which is luckier than it sounds. Fischig, looking at the traps left behind for them, starts to understand what they are actually up against — this is careful, well-resourced work, not thugs.

Aemos reports back on the name Pontius. It is a common enough name to be almost useless, except that the trail runs to the Glaw family and specifically to Pontius Glaw, who is notorious, tied to genuinely dark business, and dead. Eisenhorn's instinct says the dead man is still relevant.

Bequin is brought in, shivering. Eisenhorn finds her repellent to be near — she is an untouchable, a psychic null, which is precisely what makes her worth having and exactly why nobody wants her around. He starts thinking about using her anyway, and does not enjoy the thought.

He sets the next move: off-world, to Gudrun. Fischig comes with them, and what has been a manhunt starts to look like a team.

## 6 · The casket, and a burial

Carpel is trying to steer him, so Eisenhorn decides Fischig is the ally worth keeping close.

He sits an auto-seance with Lowink to read the psychometric traces on the evidence. Lowink goes into a semi-trance and opens to the warp, and what comes back is wrong. One item in particular — a casket — is thick with the psychic residue of people who died badly.

What Eisenhorn feels coming off it is hunger. The session breaks off early. Lowink comes out of it shaken and warns him plainly that the thing is malevolent. Eisenhorn is rattled and files it away, because there is work to do.

Then he and Betancore prepare Vibben's body and give her to the stars.

Betancore has their passage arranged: the Essene, a huge ship, and they leave Hubris behind. Tobius Maxilla welcomes them aboard.

## 7 · The Essene

Maxilla is a flamboyant, wealthy free trader who knows the routes and deals in luxury, and enjoys all three facts about himself. Eisenhorn does not warm to him, but concedes that his cooperation is worth having.

The voyage settles into routine. Betancore and the servitors overhaul the ship. Eisenhorn, Aemos, and Fischig grind through the evidence. Bequin keeps to herself with books, working out what to make of a self she has been told is abnormal.

On the third day Maxilla mentions, in passing, that Eyclone had approached him for a cargo run to Gudrun. Pressed on it, he becomes evasive, and Eisenhorn starts wondering how far into this the captain already is.

They reach the Gudrun system and find far too much naval traffic. An inspection is announced that nobody expected. Eisenhorn reads it as a trap being set, while Maxilla insists cooperation will make it painless.

The security detail comes aboard. Eisenhorn declares himself an Inquisitor. Weapons come up anyway.

## 8 · The airgate

The airgate chamber turns into a running gunfight — a dozen attackers against Maxilla's people. The detail is well armed but fights like men who have not done this much, spraying fire at Maxilla and his servants. Eisenhorn survives on movement, answering with a stub-pistol, while Maxilla reveals the sort of weaponry money buys and burns men down with it.

Eisenhorn ducks into an inspection tunnel to get out from under the guns and uses his inquisitorial rosette to take control of the docking clamp system. He crushes a pursuing trooper with it. He is hurt and badly outnumbered and wins anyway.

When it stops: Fischig is alive and injured, Maxilla is alive and much worse, cheerfully explaining how much of him is augmetic and how little he is worried.

The attackers turn out to be deserters from a military founding on Gudrun. Procurator Olm Madorthene fills in the background — men pushed into uniform who were never ready for it, which says something about the state of the system raising them.

Eisenhorn does not believe deserters organised this. Somebody with real influence pointed them at him, which means the conspiracy reaches further up than he had assumed.

They board a gun-cutter under false identity and head for Dorsay, into the Festival of Founding, past spaceports and warships that make the scale of the Imperial machine plain — and the peace it maintains look thin.

## 9 · Dorsay and Pursuing Tanokbrey

Dorsay is rich, cultured, and busy, and it is preparing a parade for its regiments. Eisenhorn is there for Effries Tanokbrey, a rogue trader who does not want to be found.

He arrives at the municipal port with Betancore, Aemos, and Bequin. The city is built of enormous stone blocks and stitched together by canals full of boats. They dress down to pass unnoticed and take a suite in an expensive hotel, where Betancore improves the security.

Aemos throws himself into gathering information. Bequin turns out to be very good at playing an aristocrat — charming, quick, and entirely convincing. At the Guild Sinesias, Eisenhorn works a meeting as a merchant out of Hesperus, asking about a xenos-cultured crop and listening for what he is really after. He and Bequin banter their way through it, and the partnership starts to click.

They find Tanokbrey in a crowded tavern. The confrontation goes physical fast, Eisenhorn improvising through it, and Tanokbrey runs — out into a chase through the canals with the festival crowds everywhere.

It ends where the military parade is forming, and the celebration turns into panic. Cornered, Tanokbrey pulls a gun, and the young conscripts of the Gudrunite Rifles shoot him down. It is the first man any of them has killed. That is what their parade day gives them.

## 10 · A Clash of Jurisdictions

Night at the Dorsay Regency. Betancore's warning wakes Eisenhorn — intruders in the building. He comes up with his power sword into a dim room and two figures who are psykers strong enough to have him beaten.

Bequin saves him. Her blankness cuts the psychic pressure dead and hands him back the room. He takes one of them, the younger accomplice. The other reveals himself: Inquisitor Commodus Voke, ancient and powerful.

They argue jurisdiction. Voke has been working Tanokbrey for a long time and it has led him to House Glaw, an old family with an ugly past. Eisenhorn is looking at House Glaw from the other direction, as part of something much wider. They are hunting the same thing, so they agree to work together despite disliking each other's methods, and Voke attaches his man Heldane to Eisenhorn to make sure of it.

The next day they arrive at the Glaw estate dressed as nobility and are received by Lady Fabrina Glaw at a grand banquet thick with influential people. Eisenhorn watches Urisel and Oberon Glaw all evening, reading them, and files what he sees.

Afterwards, in their chambers, the team goes over what they have on the estate's layout — including cellars that are not on any plan. Eisenhorn goes down alone and quietly, and finds tunnels, and a chapel with an altar that is deeply wrong.

Then Kowitz has a laspistol on him and wants to know what he thinks he is doing, and what he knows about the Pontius.

## 11 · In the Arena of Betrayal

Eisenhorn is caught inside House Glaw, with Urisel Glaw and his enforcer Gorgone Locke. He reaches for his psychic strength and finds the room shielded against exactly that. He attacks regardless, and is swarmed, taken, and manacled to a wooden cross.

Locke tortures him for answers. Eisenhorn holds, and taunts him while he is at it. The physical part is bad; the part that lands harder is what the Glaws are actually planning and how far into the Imperium the betrayal runs.

Aemos brings him round — Bequin and the others are captives too. They start working out how to get out.

The Glaws have other plans, which is entertainment. The prisoners are marched into an arena to be put against carnodons, engineered killing animals, with a crowd baying at them from the stands.

Eisenhorn fights one off. That breaks something open, and the killing spills off the sand and into the audience. In the middle of the chaos, Imperial naval forces hit the estate — Voke, arriving with overwhelming force and no hesitation at all about using it.

The chapter leaves Eisenhorn hurt, alive, and more certain of the Inquisition's reach than he was before he was nailed to a cross.
